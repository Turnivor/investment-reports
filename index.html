How to read these

Verdicts reflect the price on the research date, not today. The age under each date shows how old a verdict is, and anything older than 90 days is marked as needing a refresh. Each report lists its sources, the assumptions behind every valuation, and the questions it could not answer.

The bars under a verdict show how far the report's bear, base and bull values sit below or above the price on the research date.

"Now" is the latest price, saved once each weekday at 16:00 UTC, and how far it sits above or below the price on the research date. The scorecard checks every company verdict against it.

Scorecard

Every company verdict, checked against what the price did afterwards. It marks the research, not anyone's returns.

How a verdict is judged

Each company has a sector benchmark, chosen when its report was filed. An Avoid held up if the share did worse than its sector, and a Favour if it did better. A Wait or hold is a call on the share's own price: it held up if the shares got cheaper while waiting, and did not if they got more expensive. A gap or a move of less than 5 points counts as little change.

The benchmarks are funds or indexes that follow the sector (hover a sector name to see which). Some hold the share itself, which narrows the gap. Only price moves are counted, not dividends.

Nothing is judged in its first 90 days, because a few weeks of price movement says little about a verdict built on three-year scenarios. With this few reports it is a learning log, not a statistic. Industry and comparison reports have no single price, so they are left out.

Prices come from Yahoo Finance, in each share's own currency, saved once each weekday at 16:00 UTC. Markets still trading at that hour, such as New York, show the price at that moment rather than the close.
