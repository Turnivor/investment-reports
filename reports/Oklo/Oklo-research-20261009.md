## 2. Business Essence — Duan Yongping's "Right Business"

**In one sentence:** Oklo wants to be a **nuclear independent power producer (IPP)**. It designs, builds, owns and operates small sodium-cooled fast reactors and sells their electricity and heat under long-term contracts. Fuel recycling and isotope sales are add-ons.

### 2.1 Business model canvas

| Element | Oklo |
|---|---|
| Customer | Data centers / hyperscalers (Meta, Switch, Equinix LOI), the military (Eielson AFB), industry (Diamondback LOI), and isotope buyers |
| What they pay for | 24/7 carbon-free firm power, sited "behind the meter" or near load |
| Revenue type | **Long-term PPAs (recurring, 20+ years)**, not one-off reactor sales. That makes it more like a utility than a vendor |
| Capital intensity | **Very high.** Oklo puts the reactors on its own balance sheet. Every GW at an assumed $5,000–8,000/kW means $5–8B of capex |
| Other lines | Isotopes (Atomic Alchemy, Groves), fuel recycling (Tennessee Advanced Fuel Center), engineering services (ARMEC, Creative Engineers) |

### 2.2 Revenue structure

There is none yet. TTM revenue of $1.2M comes from acquired engineering shops. **The income statement tells you nothing about business quality.** That is the core of the analysis.

### 2.3 Unit economics (first-principles estimate, *low confidence*)

| Item | Assumption | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 GW at 92% capacity factor | ~8.1 TWh/yr | Standard nuclear |
| Price | ~$90–110/MWh | Hyperscalers pay a premium for firm clean power. Lazard-cited Aurora LCOE estimate: $80–130/MWh |
| Revenue per GW | ~$0.7–0.9B/yr | |
| Capex per GW | $5–8B (unproven) | Analyst estimate ~$4,700/kW for a 15 MW unit. First-of-a-kind (FOAK) nuclear almost always overruns |
| Resulting ROIC | ~8–12% if costs hold | That is utility-like. Good, not great |
| **Reality check** | Darlington BWRX-300 (the only disclosed SMR budget): C$20.9B / 1.2 GW ≈ **C$17,400/kW**; Natrium ≈ $11,600/kW | If Aurora lands anywhere near these, the bull case above is too generous. See the [industry report](../industry/AdvancedNuclear-SMR-industry-20261009.md) |

### 2.4 Operating leverage

Pre-revenue, so there is negative operating leverage today: $250M+ a year of opex and SBC with no revenue. Once a fleet runs, operating leverage is high (fuel is cheap, O&M is largely fixed), but capital intensity caps ROIC.

### 2.5 Peer comparison

| | Oklo | NuScale (SMR) | NANO Nuclear (NNE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market cap | ~$6.6B | $3.1B | $0.8B |
| Net cash | ~$3.1B | ~$1.1B | ~$0.58B |

Oklo is the most expensive pure-play on EV, and it still has more cash than anyone else.

> **Duan Yongping's question:** *What's good about this business, in one sentence?*
> **If it works:** contracted, 24/7, carbon-free electricity sold for decades to the world's richest customers, from plants that are cheap to run once built.
> **The catch:** "if it works" carries the whole sentence. Today it is a capital-hungry construction and regulatory project, not a business. Duan's rule is "don't do what you don't understand". **Nobody, including Oklo, yet knows what an Aurora costs to build.**

## 3. Moat Assessment — Buffett's "Economic Moat"

| Moat type | Rating | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / pricing power | ⭐⭐ | Oklo is the best-known advanced-nuclear brand among AI customers (Meta, Switch). But power is a commodity: pricing power comes from scarcity of *firm clean* power, and that is shared with Constellation, Vistra, TerraPower, X-energy, GE-Hitachi and others |
| Switching costs | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (once built) | A 20–40 year PPA plus an on-site plant is very sticky. Today: ⭐. LOIs and frameworks are non-binding |
| Network effects | ⭐ | None in the classic sense |
| Scale / learning curve | ⭐⭐ (potential) | Factory-style standard units *could* cut costs. Groves (criticality in ~11 months) is the first real evidence of execution speed. Unproven at power scale |
| Technology / regulatory | ⭐⭐⭐ | 10+ years of sodium fast-reactor design (EBR-II heritage), an NRC-approved topical report, DOE pilot authorizations, fuel recycling know-how, HALEU access. **Regulatory permits are the most real moat in nuclear**: slow and expensive for others to replicate |
| Fuel access | ⭐⭐⭐ | 5 t HALEU from INL, Centrus LOI, DOE plutonium talks. Fuel is the industry bottleneck, and Oklo is unusually well placed |

**Trend:** Over the past 2 years the moat has **widened**: a reactor is operating (Groves), DOE/NRC approvals came in, the fuel position improved and Meta signed up. Over the next 5 years the moat depends entirely on whether Aurora-INL runs in 2028 at a cost that makes sense.

> **Buffett's question:** *Will this moat still be there in 10 years? What could destroy it?*
> The regulatory and fuel moat will probably survive. What could destroy the *economic* moat: (1) FOAK cost overruns making Aurora power more expensive than gas plus storage or large reactors; (2) better-funded competitors (TerraPower, GE-Hitachi BWRX-300 with TVA/OPG, X-energy with Amazon, Kairos with Google) winning the same hyperscaler contracts; (3) a nuclear incident anywhere. **Buffett would say there is no moat yet, only a moat under construction.** He doesn't buy those.

### 4.3 Cross-checks with mental models

- **Technology adoption curve:** advanced nuclear is in the "innovators" phase. Investors are pricing it as "early majority".
- **Game theory:** hyperscalers sign many non-binding LOIs with many vendors on purpose. That is a cheap option for them, not a commitment to Oklo.
- **Capital-cycle theory:** money is flooding into SMRs (Oklo raised $1.9B this year, NuScale's share count +132% YoY). Capital floods usually precede low returns.

### 4.4 Bias check
- **Narrative bias:** "AI needs nuclear" is true, and it still doesn't mean *this* company earns a return.
- **Anchoring:** the stock is down 82% from its $194 peak. "Cheap vs. the peak" is not "cheap vs. value".
- **Survivorship bias:** we remember Tesla and forget hundreds of failed capital-intensive startups.

### 4.5 The bear case (short sellers)
- Kerrisdale (Nov 2024): "story stock" with SPAC-style inflated metrics and an unrealistic timeline. Partly wrong: Groves went critical fast. Partly still valid: no power revenue, no binding prices.
- Today's bears: 18% short interest. The argument is dilution plus a valuation that assumes success.

> **Munger's question:** *Where am I most likely wrong? Why would smart people not buy this?*
> **Where I could be wrong:** if Aurora economics come in near $4,000–5,000/kW and Meta/Switch turn into binding multi-GW PPAs, Oklo could scale far beyond my base case. Groves shows they can build fast.
> **Why smart people don't buy:** there is no price, no cost and no contract to underwrite yet. You are buying a story whose key numbers don't exist, from a company that issues ~$1B of new stock every few months.

## 6. Industry & Civilizational Trend — Li Lu's Framework

- **Is this a civilizational paradigm shift?** Probably yes, on a 20–30 year view. Electricity demand is rising again (AI data centers, electrification, reshoring), and only nuclear offers firm, dense, carbon-free power at scale. US policy (May 2025 executive orders targeting 400 GW of nuclear by 2050, DOE pilot program, NRC reform) is the most supportive in 50 years.
- **Historical analogy:** this looks more like **electrification in 1900** or **railroads in the 1850s** than the internet: a real transformation, very capital-intensive, and the infrastructure builders often did *not* capture the value (many railroads went bankrupt; investors made money buying assets after the crash).
- **TAM:** several hundred GW of new US firm capacity by 2050. Even a 5% share would be 20+ GW for one company. The ceiling is not the problem; **cost and execution are**.
- **Value-chain position:** Oklo is trying to own the *whole* chain (design → fuel → build → operate → recycle). That gives maximum value capture if it works and maximum capital need and risk if it doesn't.
- **Technology-path risk:** sodium fast reactors have a long but mixed history (EBR-II worked well; Fermi-1, Superphénix and Monju had problems). Light-water SMRs (GE-Hitachi BWRX-300) carry less tech risk; high-temperature gas reactors (X-energy) compete for the same customers.
- **Concentration:** customer concentration is high (Meta, Switch). Supplier concentration is high (HALEU: Centrus, DOE).

> **Li Lu's question:** *Twenty years from now, is this the "Standard Oil of its era" or a "flash-in-the-pan 3Com"?*
> The *industry* will very likely be a civilizational winner. Oklo has maybe a 20–25% chance of being one of its lasting leaders. It is the best-funded pure-play with real momentum (Groves), but technology, cost and dilution risk are all unresolved. Li Lu buys businesses whose winning is clear and whose price leaves a margin of safety. **Neither is true yet.**

### 7.2 Reverse DCF: what does $34.44 imply?

Using the audited terminal PE (10.7x at r=10%, g=3%, ROIC=12%) and ~3.85%/yr dilution, **a 10% annual return from $34.44 requires ~$2.25B of net income in 2036** (tool result: IRR 9.93%).

At about $0.25–0.30B of net income per operating GW, that means **~7–8 GW operating by 2036**, from **0 GW today** and a first 75 MWe unit targeted for 2028. That is roughly 100 Aurora-75 units in 8 years, or a fleet the size of ~8 large reactors. **The current price assumes near-perfect execution.**
