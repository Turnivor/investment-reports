# India Financial Services — Industry Investment Research

**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Framework:** value-chain scan + Buffett / Munger / Duan Yongping / Li Lu
**Scope:** everything that sells money, moves money or manages money in India: banks, non-bank lenders (NBFCs), insurers, asset managers, exchanges and market infrastructure, payments and fintech. Prices are NSE closes of 9 Oct 2026 unless stated. ₹1 lakh crore = ₹1 trillion ≈ US$10.5B at ~₹95.5 per dollar.
**Information richness:** **A for large banks, NBFCs, insurers, AMCs, exchanges** (decades of audited data, quarterly disclosure) · **B for new listings** (NSE, Groww, Tata Capital, HDB Financial, ICICI AMC: under 18 months of public trading) · **C for unlisted** (PhonePe, Zerodha, Razorpay)

> **AI research limitations:** "India is the next China" is one of the most repeated stories in investing, so an AI summary drifts toward the optimistic consensus. This report leans on hard numbers (reported profits, book values, penetration ratios, actual 5- and 10-year share returns) and labels forecasts as forecasts. Company figures come mostly from Screener.in and Q1 FY27 (April–June 2026) results coverage, which are secondary sources. Not investment advice.

> **Currency and your money:** this report is written in Indian rupees. HDFC Bank share ₹707 ≈ €6.51 ≈ 34.8 lei · HDFC Bank ADR $22.28 ≈ €19.86 ≈ 106 lei · ICICI Bank ADR $27.89 ≈ €24.86 ≈ 133 lei. The National Bank of Romania forecasts 6.1% for end-2026 and 3.4% for end-2027. Figures in lei and euros are conversions at that day's rate and move with it.

## 0. The short answer to the thesis

The thesis was: *India is becoming a superpower, its people will need far more financial services, so Indian financial companies should do very well.*

**The first two thirds hold up. The last third does not follow automatically.**

| Part of the thesis | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| India's economy keeps growing fast | **Right** | Real GDP grew 7.6% in FY26; FY27 forecasts 6.4–6.9% (made before the oil shock) |
| "Superpower" | **Overstated** | Large in total, poor per person: IMF puts 2026 GDP per capita at about $2,812. That poverty is exactly why the runway is long, but it also caps what each customer can pay |
| People will use far more financial services | **Right, and already happening** | Bank credit ~57% of GDP vs ~199% private debt in China; mutual funds ~21% of GDP vs 74% global average; insurance 3.7% of GDP vs 7.3% global; only 5.2 crore people hold a credit card |
| Therefore the stocks do well | **Not automatic** | HDFC Bank compounded profit 19% a year for 5 years and the share price went **down 3% a year**. Bajaj Finance: profit +34% a year, stock +4% a year. Growth was real; shareholders had already paid for it |
| A foreign investor captures it | **Leaks** | The rupee has lost roughly 3–4% a year against the dollar and euro for a decade (≈₹67 to ≈₹95.5 per dollar). A euro investor's return is the rupee return minus that |

**What makes October 2026 interesting** is that the consensus has cracked. The Nifty is down about 14–17% this year, foreign investors pulled a record ~₹2.19 lakh crore out of Indian equities by mid-May, Brent is near $100 on the Middle East conflict, and the rupee is at record lows. India's largest private bank trades at 1.8 times book, the cheapest in many years. The growth story has not changed. The price paid for it has.

## 1. Investment Logic Chain and Validation

### 1.1 The chain

```
1.4 billion people, median age under 30, real GDP growing 6–7.5% a year
    → Incomes rise from a very low base ($2,812 per head)
        → Households move from cash/gold/land to financial products
          (first loan, first card, first mutual fund SIP, first insurance policy)
            → Financial-sector revenue grows faster than GDP for decades
                → Digital rails (Aadhaar ID, UPI payments, account aggregator) cut the cost of serving small customers
                    → Winners: whoever owns (1) cheap deposits, (2) underwriting skill,
                       (3) a regulated toll booth, or (4) the customer's savings flow
                    → Losers: whoever only owns "users" on a free public rail
```

### 1.2 Link-by-link validation

| Link | Core assumption | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth → income | India keeps compounding | FY26 real GDP +7.6%; FY27 forecasts 6.4–6.9% (pre-oil-shock; RBI already cut to 6.6% in June) | ★★★★☆ |
| Income → more credit | Credit deepens | Bank credit ~57% of GDP; system credit growth 13–14% expected FY27, ~18–20% in the June 2026 quarter. Household debt already 45.5–45.8% of GDP and rising faster than assets | ★★★★☆ |
| Income → savings financialise | Money leaves gold and deposits | Mutual fund AUM ₹82.2 lakh crore (June 2026); SIP accounts 10.45 crore; demat accounts 5.5 crore (2021) → ~21.6 crore | ★★★★★ |
| → Insurance penetration rises | People buy protection | **Flat.** 3.7% of GDP in both FY24 and FY25; life slipped 2.8% → 2.7% | ★★☆☆☆ |
| → Digital rails create profit pools | Payments make money | **Volume yes, profit no.** UPI did 24.07 billion transactions worth ₹29.37 lakh crore in September 2026, but merchant fees are zero below ₹2,000 by government policy | ★★☆☆☆ |
| → Listed companies earn high returns | Growth turns into ROE | Mixed. ICICI ROA ~2.2%, Bajaj Finance ROE 20.4%, exchanges ROE 46–56%. But sector NIMs fell ~19–21 bps in FY26 and HDFC Bank's ROE is 13.6% | ★★★☆☆ |
| → Shareholders get paid | Returns reach the share price | 10-year price CAGR: ICICI 20%, Bajaj Finance 25%, HDFC Bank 8%, Kotak 11%. 5-year: HDFC Bank −3%, Kotak +2%, Bajaj Finance +4%, HDFC Life −5%, SBI Card −13% | ★★★☆☆ |

**The weak links are the last three.** Demand is not the question. The questions are who is allowed to earn a profit on it (the state sets payment fees, caps and rules), how much of the growth was pre-paid in the share price, and how much the rupee takes back.

> **Duan Yongping's question:** *Everyone agrees India will grow. What do I know that the price doesn't?*
> Until this year, nothing. In 2026 the answer may be different: the price of the largest franchise has fallen 30% from its high while its deposits grew 14.7% and its bad loans stayed at 1.17%. That is a gap between business and price, which is the only thing worth looking for.

## 2. Value-Chain Map

### 2.1 Structure

```
FUNDING (the raw material is cheap money)
  Household deposits ──► Banks (CASA = current + savings accounts, the cheapest money in India)
  Bond market / bank loans ──► NBFCs (pay 1.5–3 points more than banks for funds)

LENDING (convert money into loans)
  Private banks        HDFC Bank, ICICI, Kotak, Axis, IndusInd, IDFC First, Federal
  State banks          SBI, Bank of Baroda, PNB, Canara (≈ half of system assets)
  Small finance banks  AU SFB, Ujjivan, Equitas
  NBFCs  consumer      Bajaj Finance, HDB Financial, Tata Capital, Jio Financial
         vehicles      Shriram Finance, Cholamandalam, M&M Finance
         gold loans    Muthoot Finance, Manappuram
         housing       Bajaj Housing, LIC Housing, PNB Housing, Aavas, Aptus
         cards         SBI Cards
         micro/MSME    CreditAccess Grameen, Five-Star

PROTECTION (insurance)
  Life      LIC (state), SBI Life, HDFC Life, ICICI Prudential Life, Max Financial
  General   ICICI Lombard, Star Health, Go Digit, New India Assurance
  Broker    PB Fintech (Policybazaar)

SAVINGS AND INVESTMENT (manage the money)
  Asset managers   ICICI Prudential AMC, HDFC AMC, Nippon Life India AM, Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC, UTI
  Wealth           360 ONE, Nuvama, Anand Rathi Wealth
  Brokers          Groww, Zerodha (unlisted), Angel One, Motilal Oswal

MARKET INFRASTRUCTURE (the toll booths)
  Exchanges        NSE (listed Sep 2026), BSE, MCX (commodities)
  Depositories     CDSL, NSDL          Registrars: CAMS, KFin Technologies
  Ratings/data     CRISIL (S&P), ICRA (Moody's), CARE; credit bureau CIBIL (TransUnion, unlisted)

PAYMENTS AND FINTECH (the rails)
  Public rail      UPI, run by NPCI (not-for-profit, bank-owned): 24 billion transactions a month
  Apps             PhonePe (Walmart, unlisted), Google Pay, Paytm (listed), Navi, CRED
  Merchant         Pine Labs, Razorpay (unlisted), PayU (Prosus)
```

### 2.2 Business characteristics by segment

| Segment | How it earns | Typical return | Competition | Moat type | Cyclicality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private banks | Spread between cheap deposits and loans, plus fees | ROA 1.5–2.4%, ROE 13–17% | Oligopoly of ~5 at the top | Deposit franchise, licence, trust | Medium (credit cycle) |
| State banks | Same, with government mandates | ROA ~1%, ROE ~15% at this point of the cycle | Many, state-owned | Scale, sovereign backing | High |
| NBFCs | Borrow wholesale, lend to niches banks avoid | ROA 2.3–4.7%, ROE 15–21% | Fragmented, but a few dominate niches | Underwriting data, distribution | High (funding + credit) |
| Life insurance | Long-dated savings/protection; value is in new-business margin | VNB margin 23–28%, ROEV ~15% | LIC + 4 bank-backed privates | Bank distribution, brand | Low–medium |
| General insurance | Underwriting + float | Combined ratio 103–107%, ROE ~17% | Crowded, price wars in motor | Claims data, scale | Medium (catastrophes) |
| Asset managers | % fee on assets | ROE 33–86%, almost no capital needed | Top 5 hold most assets | Brand, distribution, performance record | Tied to market levels |
| Exchanges/depositories | Fee per trade/account | ROE 24.5–56%, operating margin 50%+ | **Duopoly / near-monopoly** | Regulatory licence + liquidity network effect | Tied to trading volume and SEBI rules |
| Payments apps | Almost nothing on UPI itself; cross-sell loans/insurance | Paytm ROE 4.6% | Two apps hold ~79% of volume, earn little | Weak: the rail is public and free | Low, but policy-dependent |

### 2.3 The chokepoints

Three places in the chain where supply is scarce, substitution is hard and returns are highest:

1. **Low-cost deposits.** India's loan-to-deposit ratio is ~82% system-wide and ~96.5% at HDFC Bank. Whoever gathers cheap savings accounts controls growth. BCG's FY27 framing is that the recovery "depends on winning the deposit war". This is why ICICI (CASA ~38–40%, NIM 4.36%) is out-earning HDFC Bank (CASA 32%, NIM 3.26–3.40%).
2. **Exchange and depository licences.** NSE and BSE are a legal duopoly; CDSL and NSDL are the only two depositories; CAMS and KFin split mutual-fund record-keeping. ROEs of 24.5–56% with no credit risk. The risk is the regulator, which has already cut options volumes once.
3. **Underwriting data in under-served niches.** Bajaj Finance (consumer durables), Shriram (used trucks), Muthoot (gold). These earn ROAs of 3–5% that no bank matches, because they price risk banks cannot.

What is **not** a chokepoint: payments. UPI is a public utility with a zero merchant fee below ₹2,000. This is the opposite of Visa and Mastercard, and it is the single biggest place where a Western investor's intuition about "India fintech" goes wrong.

> **Buffett's question:** *Which of these would I be happy to own if the stock market closed for ten years?*
> The toll booths (NSE, depositories) and the two or three banks with the cheapest deposits. Not the apps.

## 4. Four-Masters Analysis of the Leaders

### 4.1 ICICI Bank (IBN) — Information A

- **Business (Duan):** A full-service private bank that rebuilt itself after 2018 around risk-calibrated profit. Q1 FY27 standalone profit ₹14,805 crore (+15.9%); NIM 4.36%, the best of the large banks and stable through a rate-cut cycle; advances ₹16.31 lakh crore (+~19.6%); deposits ₹18.34 lakh crore (+14%); gross NPA 1.38%, net 0.35%. ROA ~2.2%. Owns listed insurers and the largest AMC.
- **Moat (Buffett):**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / trust | ★★★★☆ | Second-largest private bank, systemically important |
| Switching cost | ★★★★☆ | Salary accounts, business banking (+28%), digital stack |
| Network effect | ★★☆☆☆ | Limited |
| Scale | ★★★★☆ | ₹9.7 lakh crore market cap, national branch + digital reach |
| Licence | ★★★★★ | New universal bank licences are almost never issued |

- **Risk (Munger):** Loans growing ~20% against deposits at 14% cannot continue; either growth slows or it pays up for deposits. Rural (+35%) and business banking (+28%) are the fastest-growing and least-seasoned books. CASA has slipped (41% → ~38–40%). A bank growing this fast into a slowing economy is how every bank accident starts.
- **Management:** CEO Sandeep Bakhshi (since 2018) is widely credited with the turnaround; professional management, no promoter. **A−** (the question is succession).
- **Valuation:** P/E 17.3, P/B 2.57. Not cheap in absolute terms, fair for a 2.2% ROA bank. 1-year price change −2%.
- **Rating:** ★★★★☆ — best operating bank you can reach from Europe; priced as such.

### 4.2 HDFC Bank (HDB) — Information A

- **Business (Duan):** India's largest private bank, for 25 years the textbook compounder. The 2023 merger with its parent added a huge mortgage book funded by expensive borrowings. Since then it has been digesting. Q1 FY27 profit ₹19,060 crore (+5%; +9.8% excluding a one-off a year ago); NIM 3.26% on total assets, the lowest since the merger; advances ₹30.61 lakh crore (+15.4%); deposits ₹31.71 lakh crore (+14.7%); CASA down to 32% from 38% in 2023; gross NPA 1.17%; capital adequacy 19.6%.
- **Moat:**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / trust | ★★★★☆ | Dented in 2026 by the chairman's exit, still the default private bank |
| Switching cost | ★★★★☆ | Largest retail and SME customer base |
| Scale | ★★★★★ | ₹10.9 lakh crore market cap; largest private balance sheet |
| Deposit franchise | ★★★☆☆ | **Weakening**: CASA 32% and falling, time deposits growing 17.4% |
| Licence | ★★★★★ | Same as peers |

- **Risk (Munger):** Three things at once. (1) **Governance:** the chairman resigned on 18 March citing ethics; an Indian Express report in May alleged ~₹45 crore of disguised payments to a state road corporation to attract deposits; a US securities class action followed. The bank's outside law firms said the allegations were not substantiated and the RBI said it had "no material concerns on record". Unproven either way. (2) **Leadership:** the CEO leaves on 26 October 2026 and a new one starts. (3) **Economics:** ROE 13.6% is below what earned the old premium, and management says margin repair "will not happen quickly". Foreign holders cut their stake by 2.43 points to 39.39% in the September quarter alone.
- **Management:** In transition. **B, under review.**
- **Valuation:** P/E 13.8, P/B 1.81, dividend yield 1.84%. Share price ₹707 vs a 52-week range of ₹682–1,020. The lowest price-to-book in many years for this franchise.
- **Rating:** ★★★★☆ — the classic "great company with a temporary problem, or a good company whose greatness ended" question. Price already assumes a lot of the second.

### 4.3 State Bank of India — Information A

- **Business:** A quarter of the banking system, state-owned. Q1 FY27 profit ₹21,121 crore (+10%), NII +15%, domestic NIM 3.0%, gross NPA 1.47% (two-decade low). P/E 10.5, P/B 1.42, ROE 15.4%.
- **Moat:** Sovereign trust and unmatched deposit reach (★★★★★ scale). **Risk:** it is an instrument of government policy first and a shareholder vehicle second; this is the best point in its credit cycle in 20 years, which is when state banks look cheapest and are riskiest. **Management B.**
- **Rating:** ★★★☆☆ — cheap for a reason that returns every cycle.

### 4.4 Kotak Mahindra Bank and Axis Bank — Information A

- **Kotak:** highest NIM (4.53%) and ROA (2.14%) but ROE only ~11–12% because it holds excess capital; profit +26% in Q1; P/E 21.9, P/B 2.42; 5-year price CAGR 2%. Conservative founder-led culture (Uday Kotak now non-executive). ★★★☆☆ — quality, slow, India-only.
- **Axis:** NIM fell to 3.46% from 3.73%; ROA 1.5%, ROE ~14%; P/E 14.1, P/B 1.77. The perennial number three. ★★★☆☆.

### 4.5 Bajaj Finance — Information A

- **Business (Duan):** India's best consumer lender: 5 million new customers a quarter, AUM ₹5.47 lakh crore (+24%), Q1 profit ₹6,081 crore (+28%), ROA 4.7%, ROE 20.4%, credit cost ~1.5%, gross NPA 0.96%.
- **Moat:** Data and distribution at the point of sale (★★★★☆), cross-sell engine (★★★★☆), no deposit franchise (★★☆☆☆ on funding). **Risk:** it is the lender most exposed to exactly the borrowing the RBI worries about (non-housing retail is 58.4% of household debt); it borrows wholesale; and at 5.25x book any disappointment is punished. Ambit's target of ₹815 vs CLSA's ₹1,300 shows how wide the argument is. **Management A.**
- **Valuation:** P/E 29.2, P/B 5.25. Profit compounded 34% for 5 years while the stock returned 4% a year: the multiple has been deflating the whole time and is still the highest among lenders.
- **Rating:** ★★★☆☆ — wonderful business, full price, not reachable from Europe.

### 4.6 Vehicle and gold lenders: Shriram, Cholamandalam, Muthoot — Information A

- **Shriram Finance:** used-truck lending is a relationship business nobody else wants; Q1 profit +60% (partly one-offs), AUM ₹3.13 lakh crore (+15%), P/E 19.6, P/B 2.69. ★★★☆☆.
- **Cholamandalam:** AUM ₹2.54 lakh crore (+23%), ROA 3.7%, ROE 21.2%, P/E 23.6, P/B 4.45; Murugappa group, long clean record. ★★★☆☆.
- **Muthoot Finance:** gold loans; profit doubled in FY26 on the gold price; P/E 9.3, ROE 30.9%, but the share price has fallen from ₹4,150 to ₹2,625. A P/E of 9 on peak earnings is the market saying the earnings will not last. ★★☆☆☆ — a gold-price bet, not a financialisation bet.

### 4.7 Insurance: SBI Life, HDFC Life, ICICI Lombard, LIC — Information A

- **SBI Life** has the best distribution in the country (SBI's 22,000+ branches): APE +36%, VNB +29%, margin 26.2%, ~2.0x embedded value. ★★★☆☆.
- **HDFC Life:** APE +9%, margin 25%, ~1.8x EV, 5-year price CAGR −5%. ★★☆☆☆.
- **ICICI Lombard:** Q1 profit −46% on fire losses, combined ratio 107.2%, premium growth (7.5%) below the industry (10.9%). P/E 34.8. ★★☆☆☆.
- **LIC:** P/E 8.3, ~0.6x EV, the cheapest large financial in India, and state-run with a shrinking market share. ★★☆☆☆.
- **The sector problem (Munger):** penetration has been flat at 3.7% for two years. Regulators have repeatedly changed surrender values, commissions and tax treatment. Insurance in India is sold through banks, so the bank parent captures much of the value. The thesis link "people will buy more insurance" is the least validated in the chain.

### 4.8 Asset managers: HDFC AMC, ICICI Prudential AMC, Nippon — Information A/B

- **Business (Duan):** collect a fee on a pool that grows from both new savings (SIP flows up ~24–26% a year) and market returns. No capital needed: ROE 33–86%, most profit paid out (HDFC AMC yields 2.39%).
- **Moat:** bank-parent distribution and brand (★★★★☆). **Risk:** SEBI keeps cutting allowed fees; passive funds grow; earnings fall when the market does (they are geared to the Nifty, which is down ~15%). **Valuation:** HDFC AMC P/E 32.9, ICICI AMC 43.0, Nippon 40.9.
- **Rating:** HDFC AMC ★★★★☆ (the purest play on the single best-validated link in the chain, at the lowest multiple of the three); ICICI AMC ★★★☆☆; Nippon ★★★☆☆. All India-only.

### 4.9 Exchanges and infrastructure: NSE, BSE, MCX, CDSL, CAMS — Information A/B

- **NSE:** ~90%+ of cash equities and the world's largest derivatives exchange by contracts; listed two weeks ago at ~43x FY26 earnings, now just under the IPO price at ₹1,731. FY26 profit reportedly fell ~15% after SEBI's options curbs. Moat ★★★★★ (liquidity network effect + licence). Risk: its profits depend on retail options trading that the regulator openly wants less of. ★★★★☆ on business quality, **B** information (two weeks of trading), India-only.
- **BSE:** the challenger that took options share after the 2024–25 rule changes; profit ₹772 cr → ₹2,487 cr in two years; P/E 47.4; 5-year price CAGR 83%. Earnings this dependent on one regulatory quirk can reverse the same way. ★★☆☆☆ at this price.
- **MCX:** commodity derivatives monopoly, P/E 54.9, near its 52-week high. ★★☆☆☆ at this price.
- **CDSL:** one of two depositories; profit fell in FY26 (₹526 cr → ₹455 cr); P/E 55.9. ★★☆☆☆ at this price.
- **CAMS:** ~two-thirds of mutual-fund record-keeping; P/E 33.8, ROE 36.3%, yield 1.86%, 5-year price CAGR 2%; near its 52-week low. Fee pressure from AMC clients is the issue. ★★★☆☆.

### 4.10 Payments and fintech: Paytm, Groww, PB Fintech, Jio Financial — Information B

- **Paytm:** first profit in FY26 after years of losses and an RBI clampdown on its bank in 2024; P/E 129. ★★☆☆☆.
- **Groww:** largest retail broker by active clients; P/E 49.5, ROE 28.8%; revenue leans on derivatives trading. ★★☆☆☆.
- **PB Fintech:** the dominant online insurance marketplace; revenue +40%, profit +92%; P/E 62.6 after a 42% fall. A real network-effect business with a regulator that can change commissions. ★★★☆☆.
- **Jio Financial:** Reliance's financial arm at 1.02x book, ROE 1.2%. An option on Reliance's distribution, nothing more yet. ★★☆☆☆.

> **Munger's question:** *Where is the stupidity?*
> In paying 50–130 times earnings for businesses whose main revenue line can be rewritten by one regulatory circular, while the country's largest deposit franchise sits at 14 times.

## 5. Sector-Level Risk Assessment

### 5.1 Systemic risk list

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oil shock deepens (Brent ~$100; India imports ~85% of crude) → inflation, rate hikes, slower growth | Medium–high | High | Favour banks with strong capital and deposits; avoid wholesale-funded lenders |
| Rupee keeps sliding (≈₹90 → ₹95–96 per dollar this year alone) | High (it is the base case over decades) | Medium, cumulative | Demand 4 points a year of extra return; do not buy on rupee-return arithmetic |
| Retail credit cycle turns (household debt 45.8% of GDP, 58.4% non-housing) | Medium | High for NBFCs and cards | Bank bad loans are at a 1.8% multi-decade low: this is as good as it gets, not the norm |
| Deposit war keeps margins low for years | High | Medium | Prefer high-CASA banks (ICICI, Kotak, SBI) |
| Regulatory intervention (SEBI on options, RBI on unsecured loans, zero-fee payments, insurance commissions) | High | High for exchanges, fintech, insurers | Treat regulated monopoly profits as on loan from the regulator |
| Governance event at a leader (HDFC Bank 2026) | Has happened | Medium–high | Size positions for being wrong; watch 26 Oct handover and court filings |
| Valuation de-rating continues | Medium | Medium | Already well advanced in banks; barely started in exchanges and fintech |
| Thesis itself breaks (India stops growing) | Low | Very high | Not the main risk; the main risks are price, currency and rules |

### 5.2 Historical analogy

**China, 2006–2020.** The economy grew roughly sixfold in dollar terms and bank assets grew even faster. The large state banks' Hong Kong shares went broadly sideways for fifteen years, because growth was funded by issuing shares, loans were directed by the state, and margins were set by policy. The winners were a handful of private or quasi-private franchises (China Merchants Bank, Ping An for a time) and, above all, the platforms the state had not yet regulated, until it did (Ant Group, 2020).

**Indonesia's Bank Central Asia** is the opposite case: a private bank with the cheapest deposits in an under-banked country, which compounded for two decades.

**India itself, 2000–2020:** HDFC Bank and Kotak were among the world's best bank investments, while the state banks destroyed capital in the 2015–2018 bad-loan cycle.

**Lessons for today:**
- In a growing, under-banked economy the winner is **the private bank with the cheapest deposits and the most disciplined underwriting**, not "the sector".
- State-owned lenders capture the growth and give it back in the next credit cycle.
- Platforms earn what the state allows. India decided early that payments are a public good.
- Most investors in "country growth" themes earn less than the GDP numbers suggest, through dilution, currency and entry price.

### 5.3 Bias check

- **Narrative bias:** "1.4 billion people need banking" is a perfect story, and perfect stories get overpaid. For most of 2021–2024 that is exactly what happened.
- **Anchoring:** HDFC Bank at ₹707 looks cheap against ₹1,020. That is anchoring on the old price. The better test is 1.8x book for a 13.6% ROE, which is fair today and cheap only if ROE recovers toward 16–17%.
- **Herding:** currently in reverse. Foreigners are net sellers at a record pace. This is one of the few times in a decade that buying Indian financials is not the crowded trade.
- **AI bias:** this report covers large caps far better than small ones, and reachable ADRs get more attention than they would on merit alone.

## 6. Civilisational Trend (Li Lu)

- **Paradigm shift or passing boom?** A real, multi-decade shift. India is at roughly the income level where China was in the mid-2000s, with a working-age population still growing and a digital identity and payments layer that most rich countries lack. Financial deepening follows income with high regularity across countries.
- **Closest analogy:** the financialisation of US household savings between 1950 and 2000, and China's credit boom of 2000–2015. The first made asset managers and brokers rich; the second made borrowers and the state rich and bank shareholders much less so. India's institutional setup (independent central bank, private-sector banks, real minority-shareholder rights) is closer to the first, with elements of the second in payments and state banks.
- **End-state in 10–20 years:** 4–6 very large private banks plus SBI; 2 exchanges; 5–6 asset managers running several times today's assets; insurance still dominated by bank-owned companies; payments free.
- **Most likely winner-takes-most:** exchanges/depositories (already) and asset management (scale + distribution).
- **Most likely to be disrupted:** standalone fintech lenders and payment apps (by banks, Jio, and regulation); mid-sized banks with no deposit edge (by consolidation, increasingly foreign-led: SMBC–Yes Bank, Emirates NBD–RBL).

> **Li Lu's question:** *Is this within my circle of competence, and is the price giving me a margin of safety?*
> The trend is easy. Indian bank accounting, promoter structures and regulation are not. A foreign individual's edge here is patience and entry price, not information. That argues for the two or three simplest, largest, most scrutinised franchises, or an index fund, and for buying when foreigners are leaving.

### What the four would say

> **Buffett:** "A bank is a good business if it doesn't do dumb things on the asset side and has a cost advantage on the liability side. India has two or three of those. I would want to own them at a price where I don't need the growth to be right."

> **Munger:** "Everybody knew India would grow, and for five years the shareholders of the best bank in the country made nothing. Remember that before you fall in love with a GDP forecast. Then notice that the people who loved it at three times book are selling it to you at under two."

> **Duan Yongping:** "Free payments are a wonderful product and a terrible business. Lending cheap deposits to people you understand is a boring product and a wonderful business. Buy the business."

> **Li Lu:** "The trend is real and long. The discipline is to wait for the moments when foreigners are afraid of the currency, the oil price and a scandal all at once. This looks like one of those moments. It can last longer than you expect, so buy slowly."
