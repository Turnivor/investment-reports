**Builds on:** [India Financial Services industry research](IndiaFinancials-industry-20261011.md). Prices are NSE closes of 9 Oct 2026; market caps in ₹ lakh crore (×~10.5 for US$ billions at ~₹95.5 per dollar).
**Investor frame:** European individual, no existing holdings in the sector. Foreign individuals cannot buy on NSE/BSE directly, so every name is tagged **Reachable** (NYSE ADR, London GDR, or via UCITS ETF only) and the final answer is given twice: best businesses, and best reachable vehicles.

> **AI research limitations:** Large caps and ADR-listed banks have far more English-language coverage than small NBFCs, so this funnel is biased toward them. Rankings use the hard tests and moat evidence below, and the result is checked against that bias in section 5.4. Company numbers are from Screener.in and Q1 FY27 (April–June 2026) results coverage. ## 2. Layer 2 — Five hard tests → 10 survivors

### 2.1 The tests, adapted

Two of the standard five tests do not work for financial companies: a bank's "debt" is its raw material (deposits), and operating cash flow is not comparable to profit. They are replaced with the equivalents a bank analyst uses.

| # | Standard test | Used here | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P/E reasonable | P/E against growth and own history; P/B against ROE for lenders | Not above ~25x unless growth above 20% |
| 2 | ROE > 15% or improving | Same; for lenders also ROA | ROE ≥ 15%, **or** ROA ≥ 1.8% with excess capital |
| 3 | Cash flow / profit > 70% | **Earnings quality:** bad loans and credit cost (lenders); cash conversion and payout (fee businesses) | Gross NPA < 2% for banks; no one-off-driven profit |
| 4 | Debt ratio < 60% | **Funding and capital strength:** deposit franchise or capital adequacy (lenders); debt-free (fee businesses) | Capital ≥ 15%, stable funding |
| 5 | Moat ≥ ★★★ | Same | ≥ ★★★ |

## 3. Layer 3 — Fine analysis of the 10

### ICICI Bank (ICICIBANK · NYSE: IBN)

**Business in one line:** takes deposits at ~4–5% and lends at ~9%, to households, small businesses and companies, with fee income on top.
**Financial quality:** Q1 FY27 profit ₹14,805 crore (+15.9%); NIM 4.36% (stable through 125 bps of rate cuts); advances +~19.6%, deposits +14%; ROA ~2.2%; CET1 16.19%. Five-year profit CAGR 24%.
**Moat:** deposit franchise + licence + a digital stack that has taken business-banking share (+28%). Likely intact in five years.
**Top risks:** (1) loans growing 5–6 points faster than deposits; (2) the fastest-growing books (rural +35%, business banking +28%) are the least seasoned; (3) reported credit cost of 0.32% was flattered by a one-off recovery, normal is ~0.5%.
**Valuation:** P/E 17.3, P/B 2.57 against a 13-year median of ~2.9 and a low of 1.51. **Fair.**
**Finalist?** **Yes** — the high-certainty slot.

### HDFC Bank (HDFCBANK · NYSE: HDB)

**Business in one line:** the same business as ICICI at 1.9 times the loan book, still absorbing the 2023 merger with its mortgage parent.
**Financial quality:** Q1 FY27 profit ₹19,060 crore (+5% reported, +9.8% underlying); NIM 3.26% (post-merger low); advances +15.4%, deposits +14.7%; GNPA 1.17%; capital 19.6%; ROE 13.6%.
**Moat:** largest private customer base and branch network. The deposit advantage that defined it has narrowed (CASA 38% → 32% in three years).
**Top risks:** (1) unresolved governance questions after the chairman's March resignation, and a US class action; (2) new CEO from 26 October; (3) margin repair is slow by management's own account.
**Valuation:** P/E 13.8, P/B 1.81 on Screener's book value (GuruFocus, on a different book-value basis, shows the ADR near a 10-year low against a 10-year median of 3.57). **Cheap against its own history; fair if ROE never recovers.**
**Finalist?** **Yes** — the medium-certainty, re-rating slot.

### State Bank of India (SBIN · London GDR)

**Business:** the state's bank; 22,000+ branches; owns SBI Life, SBI Cards, SBI Funds.
**Quality:** profit ₹21,121 crore (+10%), NII +15%, NIM 3.0%, GNPA 1.47%. ROE 15.4%.
**Moat:** deposits nobody can match; no pricing freedom when the government needs something.
**Risks:** (1) this is the best the credit cycle gets; (2) policy lending; (3) periodic dilution.
**Valuation:** P/E 10.5, P/B 1.42. **Cheap, as it always is at this point in the cycle.**
**Finalist?** No. Peak-cycle earnings at a state lender are the classic value trap. Worth knowing as the only other large reachable name.

### Kotak Mahindra Bank (KOTAKBANK)

**Business:** the most conservative of the private banks, with strong wealth and broking arms.
**Quality:** NIM 4.53%, ROA 2.14%, profit +26%, GNPA 1.18%; ROE only ~11–12% because of surplus capital.
**Risks:** (1) slow growth by choice; (2) life after the founder; (3) P/E 21.9 for that growth.
**Valuation:** P/B 2.42. **Fair to slightly dear.**
**Finalist?** No. Lower return on equity than ICICI at a higher multiple, and not reachable.

### Bajaj Finance (BAJFINANCE)

**Business:** lends at the shop counter and through its app to 100 million+ customers, then cross-sells.
**Quality:** AUM ₹5.47 lakh crore (+24%), profit +28%, ROA 4.7%, ROE 20.4%, GNPA 0.96%.
**Risks:** (1) most exposed to the non-housing retail borrowing the RBI flags; (2) wholesale funding; (3) 5.25x book.
**Valuation:** P/E 29.2. Five-year profit CAGR 34% against a 4% share-price CAGR shows what paying too much does. **Dear.**
**Finalist?** No — on price, and not reachable. The best lender in India on operating numbers.

### Cholamandalam (CHOLAFIN)

**Business:** vehicle, home and small-business loans in semi-urban India; Murugappa family group.
**Quality:** AUM +23%, profit +46%, ROA 3.7%, ROE 21.2%.
**Risks:** cyclical borrowers; wholesale funding; 4.45x book.
**Valuation:** P/E 23.6. **Fair for the growth.**
**Finalist?** No. Good, but the same cycle risk as Bajaj Finance with a narrower moat.

### HDFC AMC (HDFCAMC)

**Business:** charges a percentage fee on mutual-fund assets gathered largely through HDFC Bank's branches and independent distributors.
**Quality:** FY26 revenue ₹4,611 crore, profit ₹2,859 crore (a 62% net margin), ROE 32.9%, debt-free, pays most of its profit out (yield 2.39%). Five-year profit CAGR 17%, ten-year 20%.
**Moat:** brand, parent distribution, long equity-fund record. Durable, with fee rates drifting down.
**Risks:** (1) SEBI fee cuts; (2) shift to passive funds; (3) earnings fall with the market.
**Valuation:** P/E 32.9, the lowest of the listed AMCs (ICICI AMC 43.0, Nippon 40.9), and the share price is near its 52-week low (₹2,262 vs ₹2,206–2,967). **Fair.**
**Finalist?** **Yes** — the growth slot: the cleanest listed expression of the best-validated link in the thesis (savings moving into funds).

### NSE

**Business:** takes a fee on every share and derivative traded on India's main exchange.
**Quality:** FY26 profit reportedly down ~15% after the options curbs; listed at ~43x FY26 earnings; trades at ₹1,731 against the ₹1,785 offer price.
**Moat:** the strongest in the sector (liquidity attracts liquidity, plus a licence).
**Risks:** (1) a regulator that wants less retail options trading; (2) BSE taking options share; (3) two weeks of public history.
**Valuation:** ~41–43x. **Dear for falling earnings.**
**Finalist?** No, **watch**. The best business in the pool; wait for a few quarters of public reporting and a lower price.

### CAMS

**Business:** keeps the investor records for roughly two-thirds of mutual-fund assets and bills the AMCs.
**Quality:** FY26 revenue ₹1,516 crore (+7%), profit ₹472 crore (+2%); ROE 36.3%; yield 1.86%.
**Risks:** (1) its customers are five large AMCs with pricing power over it; (2) profit growth has stalled; (3) technology substitution.
**Valuation:** P/E 33.8, near its 52-week low. **Fair.**
**Finalist?** No. HDFC AMC captures the same trend with pricing power on its side of the table.

### SBI Life (SBILIFE)

**Business:** sells life insurance and savings policies through SBI's branches.
**Quality:** APE +36% (helped by lumpy group business), VNB +29%, margin 26.2%, embedded value ₹85,290 crore.
**Risks:** (1) sector penetration flat at 3.7%; (2) repeated regulatory changes to product economics; (3) the bank parent can renegotiate commissions.
**Valuation:** ~2.0x embedded value. **Fair.**
**Finalist?** No. The segment is the least validated link in the chain.

## 4. Layer 4 — Four-masters deep dive

### 4.1 ICICI Bank

**Duan Yongping — the business.** ICICI rents out other people's money. The whole game is to pay less for the money than competitors and to lose less of it than competitors. On both counts it is currently the best large bank in India: NIM of 4.36% that barely moved while the central bank cut rates by 125 basis points, and normalised credit cost of about 0.5%. Its "ben fen" (doing the right thing in the right way) since 2018 has been an explicit rule that profit must be risk-calibrated, after a decade in which the bank chased corporate loan growth and paid for it with a bad-loan crisis and the departure of its previous CEO. The question is whether loan growth near 20% is consistent with that rule. Management's defence is that the growth is granular (small businesses, rural, retail) and funded by a deposit base still growing 14%. The counter-argument is that every bank says this in year three of a boom.

**Buffett — the moat.**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / pricing power | ★★★★☆ | Holds margin through a rate-cut cycle while peers lose 20 bps |
| Switching cost | ★★★★☆ | Salary and business current accounts; integrated app for payments, loans, investing |
| Network effect | ★★☆☆☆ | Limited to its merchant and supply-chain platforms |
| Scale | ★★★★☆ | ₹16.3 lakh crore loan book; cost advantage in technology spend |
| Licence | ★★★★★ | Universal bank licences are effectively closed |

Ten years out, the licence and scale will still be there. The margin advantage depends on the deposit mix, which has slipped from 41% to ~38–40% low-cost accounts in a year. **Margin of safety:** modest. At 2.57x book and 17x earnings the price assumes the current ROA persists. The comfort is in the business quality and 16.19% core capital, not in the price.

**Munger — how it fails.**
1. **Growth outruns underwriting.** Rural +35% and business banking +28% season badly in a slowdown; with oil near $100 a slowdown is plausible.
2. **Deposit squeeze.** Loans +20% against deposits +14% forces either slower growth or dearer funding; the 4.36% margin falls toward 4%.
3. **Succession.** The CEO who rebuilt the bank has been reappointed only to October 2028. ICICI's last succession (2018) happened in a scandal.

*Worst case, simply:* at its 13-year low multiple of ~1.5x book the shares would be about ₹790, roughly 40% below today, before the rupee.
*Why smart people pass:* it is a consensus "quality" holding at a consensus multiple; there is no mispricing to exploit, only a good business to hold.
*Integrity:* no current issues identified. The 2018 episode is the reminder that this board has failed before.

**Li Lu — the long view.** Financial deepening in a country at $2,812 per head is a multi-decade shift, and private banks with deposit franchises have been the main beneficiaries in every comparable case (Bank Central Asia in Indonesia, HDFC Bank itself in 2000–2020). The end-state is four to six very large private banks; ICICI is plainly one of them. Not winner-takes-all, but winners-take-most.

**Overall**
```
Rating:            ★★★★☆
Position type:     Core
Valuation zone:    Fair today (P/B 2.57). Clearly attractive below ~2.2x book,
                   about ₹1,160 / ~$24 per ADR, which is near the 52-week low area ($25.08)
Share of theme:    35–45%
Watch:             NIM below 4.1%; CASA below 36%; gross NPA rising two quarters running;
                   credit growth still >18% a year from now with deposits <14%
```

### 4.2 HDFC Bank

**Duan Yongping — the business.** For 25 years HDFC Bank was the simplest great business in India: gather the cheapest deposits in the country through relentless branch execution, lend them carefully, repeat. The 2023 merger changed the shape. It doubled the mortgage book and brought ₹6 lakh crore of market borrowings in place of cheap deposits. The bank has spent three years refilling the deposit side: deposits +14.7%, but mostly through term deposits (+17.4%), so the cheap-money share fell from 38% to 32% and the margin to a post-merger low of 3.26%. Is it still a good business? At a 13.6% ROE it is an ordinary one. Whether it becomes a good one again depends on a single variable, cost of funds, which management calls "the single biggest opportunity on margins" and also says will not improve quickly. On "ben fen": the allegation that the bank disguised payments to a state corporation to win deposits, if true, would be exactly the kind of shortcut a bank takes when it is desperate for deposits. It is unproven. The bank's two outside law firms found the former chairman's allegations unsubstantiated, and the RBI said it had no material concerns on record.

**Buffett — the moat.**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / trust | ★★★★☆ | Still the default private bank; dented in 2026 |
| Switching cost | ★★★★☆ | Largest retail, SME and card customer base |
| Network effect | ★★☆☆☆ | Merchant acquiring leader |
| Scale | ★★★★★ | ₹30.6 lakh crore advances; ₹10.9 lakh crore market cap |
| Deposit franchise | ★★★☆☆ | Was ★★★★★; CASA 32% and still falling |

In ten years the scale and licence remain. Whether the deposit advantage returns is the investment question. **Margin of safety:** real, for the first time in many years. 1.81x book and 13.8x earnings for a bank with 1.17% bad loans and 19.6% capital. If ROE stays at 13–14%, that is roughly fair value and little is lost. If ROE recovers toward 16–17% the multiple has room to rise on a growing book value. The asymmetry comes from the price, not from certainty about the outcome.

**Munger — how it fails.**
1. **The allegations are substantiated at senior level.** Fines are small next to the capital base; the damage would be to trust, regulatory freedom and management time, at a bank that already needs all three.
2. **The deposit franchise never comes back.** UPI and salary-account competition have made cheap deposits structurally scarcer for everyone; ROE settles at 13–14% and the "premium bank" becomes a utility.
3. **A new CEO cleans house.** New leaders often take large provisions in their first quarters. The handover is on 26 October.

*Worst case, simply:* a permanently ordinary bank at ~1.5x book would be about ₹585, roughly 17% below today.
*Why smart people are selling:* foreign institutions cut their holding by 2.43 points to 39.39% in a single quarter. They see a governance question with no end date, a leadership change, the worst margins among large private peers, and a falling rupee on top. Those are facts, not sentiment.
*Integrity:* open question. This is the main reason the position cannot be sized like ICICI's.

**Li Lu — the long view.** Same civilisational trend as ICICI, same end-state. The specific question here is whether a franchise can lose its edge and regain it. Banks that kept their branch network, capital and customer base through a bad patch usually have (ICICI itself after 2018 is the closest example in the same market). Banks whose problem was culture often have not.

**Overall**

### 4.3 HDFC AMC

**Duan Yongping — the business.** People hand over savings every month by standing instruction, and the company keeps a fraction of a percent of the pile every year. No inventory, no credit risk, almost no capital: ₹2,859 crore of profit on ₹4,611 crore of revenue. Industry SIP contributions grew ~24–26% last year and mutual-fund assets are still only ~21% of GDP against a global average of 74%. It is hard to design a better business. Its "ben fen" is investment performance and not over-charging; the regulator enforces the second.

**Buffett — the moat.**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / trust | ★★★★☆ | Among the oldest equity-fund records in India |
| Switching cost | ★★★☆☆ | Tax on switching and inertia of standing instructions |
| Network effect | ★☆☆☆☆ | None |
| Scale | ★★★★☆ | Fixed research cost over a large asset base: 62% net margin |
| Distribution / licence | ★★★★☆ | HDFC Bank branches plus the independent adviser network |

Ten years out the business will be several times larger in assets and somewhat lower in fee rate. **Margin of safety:** thin on the multiple (32.9x), better on the facts that the shares sit near their 52-week low, the multiple is the lowest in the peer group, and a 2.39% dividend is paid while waiting.

**Munger — how it fails.**
1. **Fee compression.** SEBI has cut allowed expense ratios before and passive funds are growing; revenue per rupee managed keeps falling.
2. **A long bear market.** Assets fall, SIPs get cancelled, and a 33x multiple contracts at the same time. The Nifty is already down ~15% this year and foreign selling has been met so far by domestic SIP money; if that money stops, this company feels it first.
3. **Parent problems.** The HDFC Bank brand is the distribution channel.

*Worst case, simply:* 20x on flat earnings is about ₹1,340, roughly 40% below today.
*Why smart people pass:* the growth is fully visible, so it is rarely cheap; and for a European investor it cannot be bought directly.

**Li Lu — the long view.** The movement of household savings from gold, property and bank deposits into securities is one of the most reliable patterns in economic development (the US from the 1950s, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, now China). Asset managers with distribution are the natural toll collectors. Scale economics favour the top five; not winner-takes-all.

                   equity market share; Q2 results on 15 Oct 2026

### 5.3 Where the sector stands

- **Valuation:** two markets in one. Large banks trade at 10–17x earnings; the Nifty Bank P/E was 13.95 in July against a five-year median of 16.13. Exchanges, brokers, fintech and asset managers trade at 33–130x.
- **Flows:** foreigners are record net sellers (~₹2.19 lakh crore out by mid-May; foreign holding in HDFC Bank down 2.43 points last quarter); domestic institutions and monthly SIP money are absorbing it. Nifty down ~14–17% this year; rupee near record lows; Brent near $100.
- **Stage:** early-to-middle expansion for the industry (penetration ratios a third to a half of global norms), late-cycle for asset quality (bad loans at a multi-decade low of 1.8%), mid-correction for share prices.
