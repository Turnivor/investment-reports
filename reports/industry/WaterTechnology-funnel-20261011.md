## 3. Stage 3: close analysis of the eight

Figures are from the stage 1 table (StockAnalysis, latest fiscal year). Business descriptions and moat judgments draw on general knowledge of each company and were not re-verified against 2026 filings, except for Pentair.

### Veralto (VLTO) · $23.6bn

- **Business:** water-quality testing instruments and chemicals (Hach), industrial water treatment (ChemTreat, Trojan UV), plus a product-marking business (Videojet). Spun out of Danaher in 2023. About 60% of sales are water.
- **Financials:** revenue +6.0%, gross margin 59.9%, operating margin 23.2%, ROE 36.5%, cash conversion 112%.
- **Moat:** Hach instruments lock in sales of their own reagents, which utilities must use for regulatory reporting. A razor-and-blade model with a regulated customer.
- **Risks:** liabilities/assets at 60% and rising to 63.8% in the latest quarter. Paying about 17 times EBITDA for Cleanwater1 ($465m). 40% of sales are not water.
- **Valuation:** P/E 24.4, forward 21.5. Down 3% this year. Reasonable for the quality.
- **Finalist?** Yes. Highest quality on the list at a mid-range price.

### Geberit (GEBN.SW) · $20.6bn

- **Business:** behind-the-wall sanitary systems, piping and bathroom ceramics, sold through plumbers across Europe.
- **Financials:** revenue +2.5%, gross margin 73.6%, operating margin 24.3%, ROE 42.4%, cash conversion 142%.
- **Moat:** plumbers are trained on Geberit systems and specify them by habit. Very strong in German-speaking Europe.
- **Risks:** European construction cycle. Slow growth. Liabilities/assets 61%.
- **Valuation:** P/E 28.0, forward 26.1. Down 15% this year. Still full for 2.5% growth.
- **Finalist?** No. Excellent company, but the price asks a lot for the growth, and the Swiss listing adds currency risk for a dollar or euro investor.

### Watts Water Technologies (WTS) · $11.8bn

- **Business:** valves, backflow preventers, drains and water-quality products for commercial and residential buildings.
- **Financials:** revenue +8.3%, gross margin 49.4%, operating margin 19.3%, ROE 18.3%, liabilities/assets 30%.
- **Moat:** products are written into plumbing codes and specified by engineers. Long record of small, sensible acquisitions.
- **Risks:** commercial construction. A family-controlled share structure.
- **Valuation:** P/E 30.8, forward 26.5, after rising 28% this year. The market has already rewarded it.
- **Finalist?** No. Right company, wrong moment.

### Badger Meter (BMI) · $3.8bn

- **Business:** smart water meters, radios and software for North American utilities.
- **Financials:** revenue +10.9%, gross margin 41.7%, operating margin 20.0%, ROE 21.5%, liabilities/assets 27% (net cash).
- **Moat:** a utility that installs a metering system keeps it for 15 to 20 years. Software revenue is growing.
- **Risks:** utility project timing (Xylem reported meter delays in 2026). Small company, high expectations.
- **Valuation:** P/E 30.8, forward 27.6, even after falling 25% this year.
- **Finalist?** Watch list. The fastest grower of the eight, but the most expensive.

### A. O. Smith (AOS) · $7.7bn

- **Business:** water heaters and boilers in North America, water treatment, plus businesses in China and India.
- **Financials:** revenue +0.3%, gross margin 38.8%, operating margin 19.0%, ROE 29.2%, cash conversion 114%, liabilities/assets 41%.
- **Moat:** a leading share of the North American water-heater market, where most sales are replacements of failed units. Strong plumber and wholesaler relationships.
- **Risks:** no growth at present. China has been shrinking. Heat-pump regulation changes the product mix. Family-controlled share structure.
- **Valuation:** P/E 15.7, forward 14.5. Down 16% this year. Low for a 29% ROE.
- **Finalist?** Yes. Highest return on equity among the cheap names.

### Mueller Water Products (MWA) · $3.4bn

- **Business:** fire hydrants, gate valves and leak detection for water utilities.
- **Financials:** revenue +8.7%, gross margin 36.4%, operating margin 19.1%, ROE 21.4% and rising (12.4% two years ago), cash conversion 144%.
- **Moat:** a very large installed base of hydrants and valves in US cities, where utilities standardize on one make.
- **Risks:** exposure to US municipal budgets as federal infrastructure money runs down. Brass and scrap costs. Foundry operations.
- **Valuation:** P/E 15.3, forward 14.1. Down 9% this year.
- **Finalist?** Watch list. Cheap and improving, but the most exposed of the eight to the US funding cliff.

### Advanced Drainage Systems (WMS) · $9.5bn

- **Business:** plastic stormwater pipe and septic systems.
- **Financials:** revenue +5.0%, operating margin 22.7%, ROE 25.1% but falling (51% two years ago), cash conversion 153%, liabilities/assets 58%.
- **Moat:** scale. Plastic pipe keeps taking share from concrete and steel. Uses recycled plastic as a cost advantage.
- **Risks:** construction cycle, resin prices, ROE trend.
- **Valuation:** P/E 21.5, forward 19.8. Down 13% this year.
- **Finalist?** No. It is a stormwater and construction business more than a water-technology one, and returns are trending down.

### Pentair (PNR) · $8.4bn

- **Business:** pool equipment, filtration and ice machines, pumps.
- **Financials:** revenue +2.3% in 2025, operating margin 20.5% (GAAP), ROE 17.5%, cash conversion 116%. 2026 guidance cut in July after pool distributors cut inventory.
- **Moat:** installed base and a three-player pool market. Weakened by admitted share loss in 2026.
- **Risks:** pool share loss, forecasting credibility, new debt for the Taco deal, class actions.
- **Valuation:** P/E 13.5, forward 10.9. Down 50% this year. Cheapest of the eight.
- **Finalist?** Yes, as the high-risk, high-upside slot. Full analysis in the [Pentair research report](../Pentair/Pentair-research-20261011.md).

## 4. Stage 4: the three finalists through four lenses

The framework picks finalists for balance, not by ranking: one high-certainty holding, one middle case, one high-risk case.

### 4.1 Veralto: the high-certainty core

**Duan Yongping, the business.** Veralto sells the instruments a water utility uses to prove its water is safe, and then sells the chemicals those instruments consume, every day, for decades. It is a good business because the customer is required by law to test, cannot easily switch instrument families, and spends very little on this compared with the cost of getting it wrong. The second half of the company (printing codes on packaging) has the same pattern of equipment followed by consumables.

**Buffett, the moat.**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / pricing power | ★★★★★ | Gross margin 59.9%, the highest of any pure water name on the list |
| Switching costs | ★★★★★ | Instruments tied to proprietary reagents and to regulatory methods |
| Network effects | ★ | None of note |
| Scale | ★★★★ | Global service and distribution |
| Technology / regulation | ★★★★ | Methods accepted by regulators |

In ten years the moat is very likely intact. Margin of safety at 21.5 times forward earnings is thin: this is a fair price for a very good business, not a bargain.

**Munger, how it fails.** Three paths: overpaying for acquisitions now that it is independent (the Cleanwater1 deal at about 17 times EBITDA is a warning), leverage creeping up (liabilities/assets went from 59.6% to 63.8% in half a year), and the non-water half dragging growth. Smart people stay away because 21 times earnings for 6% growth leaves little room. Worst case, simple arithmetic: 16 times a flat earnings base would be about 25% below today's price.

**Li Lu, the long view.** Water testing is a permanent need that grows with regulation. PFAS rules add a new thing to measure, although the US rules were partly relaxed in 2026. It is not a winner-takes-all market, but the leader keeps its place for a very long time.

```
Rating: ★★★★☆
Role: core
Entry: acceptable at the current price, better on a 10% pullback
Weight within a water allocation: 45 to 55%
Watch: liabilities/assets above 65%, acquisition multiples, organic growth below 4%
```

### 4.2 A. O. Smith: the cheap, high-return satellite

**Duan Yongping, the business.** When a water heater fails, the household has no hot water and replaces it within days, usually with whatever brand the plumber carries. A. O. Smith is one of the brands the plumber carries. That is a good business: demand is non-discretionary and repeat, and the buyer is not price-shopping. It is also a slow business, with sales flat in 2025.

**Buffett, the moat.**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / pricing power | ★★★★ | ROE 29%, operating margin 19% in a mature product |
| Switching costs | ★★★ | Wholesaler and plumber relationships, not contracts |
| Network effects | ★ | None |
| Scale | ★★★★ | A leading North American share, concentrated industry |
| Technology / regulation | ★★★ | Efficiency rules favour large makers |

In ten years people will still need hot water. The margin of safety is the price: 14.5 times forward earnings and liabilities at only 41% of assets.

**Munger, how it fails.** China keeps shrinking and takes years to stop being a drag. Regulation pushes the market toward heat pumps, where its lead is smaller. Management spends on acquisitions to find growth (it paid $470m for Leonard Valve, about 12 times EBITDA). Smart people stay away because there is no growth. Worst case: 11 times flat earnings, about 25% below today.

**Li Lu, the long view.** A mature, consolidated industry. Not a paradigm shift. The upside is India and water treatment, which are small. A steady compounder if bought cheaply, not a growth story.

```
Rating: ★★★★☆
Role: satellite
Entry: reasonable at the current price
Weight within a water allocation: 25 to 35%
Watch: North American volumes, China losses, margin holding near 19%
```

### 4.3 Pentair: the turnaround

**Duan Yongping, the business.** A replacement business at a 25% margin that got greedy on price. The business model is sound. The "do the right thing by the customer" part failed in 2025 and has to be rebuilt.

**Buffett, the moat.** ★★★ overall: brand and pricing power ★★★ (was ★★★★), switching costs ★★★, scale ★★★, network effects ★★, technology ★★. The margin of safety is the price: 10.9 times forward earnings and an 8% free cash flow yield.

**Munger, how it fails.** Share loss in pool equipment continues. Pool margins reset lower for good. The Taco debt meets a weak housing market. Smart people stay away because management was surprised by its own largest customer and went through three finance chiefs in a year. Worst case in the research report: about $30 in today's money.

**Li Lu, the long view.** The installed base of pools and buildings is durable. The market is pricing a channel correction as if it were permanent. That is either the opportunity or the trap, and pool market share over the next year will show which.

```
Rating: ★★★☆☆
Role: turnaround / option
Entry: wait for Q3 results (23 Oct 2026); more attractive below about $47
Weight within a water allocation: 10 to 20%
Watch: a third guidance cut, pool margin below 30%, insider buying, the new CFO's first guidance
```

### 5.3 Where the industry stands

- **Valuation:** the water funds trade at 21 to 24 times earnings. No historical series was found, so a percentile cannot be given. Individual leaders are at multi-year lows: Xylem at 17.5 times forward earnings against 25 to 44 times at the last five year-ends, Pentair at 10.9 against a five-year average of 18.4.
- **Money flows:** roughly flat. As of June 2026, PHO had taken in about $22m and FIW had lost about $47m for the year.
- **Stage:** mature, with pockets of growth (data-centre cooling, ultrapure water for chip factories, smart metering, PFAS treatment).
- **Tailwinds:** ageing pipes (the US EPA estimates $1.25tn of need over 20 years), data-centre demand, active deal-making. 436 water deals completed in 2025 at an average of about 10.8 times EBITDA.
- **Headwinds:** the US infrastructure law's water funding ended on 30 Sep 2026 and the 2027 budget proposes cutting the state revolving funds by about 90%. US PFAS rules were partly relaxed in 2026. New pool construction is near 58,000 a year against 75,000 to 100,000 at the peak. China is shrinking.
