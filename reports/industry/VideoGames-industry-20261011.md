# Video Games: Industry Investment Research

**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Framework:** value-chain scan + Buffett / Munger / Duan Yongping / Li Lu
**Scope:** everyone who makes, sells or carries video games: publishers and studios, console and store owners, mobile game companies, engines and advertising tools, hardware and retail. Prices are closes of 9 Oct 2026 unless stated.
**Why now:** Grand Theft Auto VI launches on 19 November 2026. This report asks whether that makes the industry a good place to invest, and where in the chain the money ends up. Companion report: [Take-Two Interactive research](../Take-Two/Take-Two-research-20261011.md).
**Information richness:** **A for the large US, Japanese and Hong Kong listings** (decades of filings, wide coverage) · **B for Korean and mainland Chinese companies** (good filings, thin English coverage; figures here come from data aggregators) · **C for the private companies** that matter most on PC and mobile (Valve, Epic Games, miHoYo, Scopely, and Electronic Arts since August 2026)

> **AI research limitations:** an AI finds far more material on large, English-language companies, so it tends to recommend them. Three of the best businesses in this industry (Valve, Epic, miHoYo) cannot be bought at all, and several of the cheapest are in Korea and China where this report relies on aggregator data. Only Take-Two received a full valuation. For every other company the figures are a snapshot: enough to rank, not enough to buy on. Not investment advice.

## 0. The short answer to the thesis

The thesis was: *the biggest game ever made is about to launch, so game companies should be a good investment.*

| Part of the thesis | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| GTA VI will be very large | **Right** | Take-Two guides to $8.0 to $8.2bn of bookings, up about 20%. Analyst forecasts run from 35 to 70 million copies in the first months. |
| It lifts the whole industry | **Partly** | Newzoo expects console revenue of $46.9bn in 2026, up 5.1%, and says it would fall without GTA VI. One game is holding up a segment. |
| The industry is growing fast | **No** | $213.9bn in 2026, up 6.1%. Players grow 3 to 4% a year. Mobile, the largest part, saw in-app spending fall 2% in the first half of 2026. |
| So the shares do well | **Not as a group** | The two sector funds are down 16% and 17% in a year. The S&P 500 is up 16%. Over five years one fund returned +53% and the other -2%, against +78% for the index. |
| So buy the company that makes it | **Not at this price** | See the Take-Two report: about $168 of base value against a $213.44 price. |

**What makes October 2026 interesting** is that the sector is out of favour. Console prices are rising because memory chips are scarce, Roblox has lost almost two thirds of its value, a Google demonstration of AI-generated game worlds knocked the whole group in January, and Chinese and Korean publishers trade at 8 to 14 times earnings. A hit-driven industry is rarely a comfortable place to invest. It is a better place to look when nobody wants it.

## 1. Investment logic chain and validation

### 1.1 The chain

```
Games are the largest paid entertainment medium ($214bn a year)
    → The number of players has almost stopped growing (3 to 4% a year)
        → Growth now comes from spending per player, inside games people already play
            → Time and money pile into a few "forever" franchises
              (Grand Theft Auto, Call of Duty, Fortnite, Honor of Kings, Mario, NBA 2K)
                → Making a new one costs more every year, the shops keep about 30%,
                  and consoles are getting dearer because of the memory shortage
                    → Winners: owners of old franchises with strict budgets,
                       and the platforms that collect the 30%
                    → Losers: mid-size publishers without a franchise, mobile studios
                       that rent their players through advertising, hardware assemblers
```

### 1.2 Link-by-link validation

| Link | Assumption | Evidence | Holds? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large market → slow player growth | The audience is close to saturated | Newzoo: players +4.4% in 2026, then 3.2% a year to 2029 | Yes |
| Slow growth → spending per player | Revenue grows faster than players | Market +6.1% against players +4.4%. But Sensor Tower counts mobile in-app spending at about $40bn in H1 2026, **down 2%**, with downloads down 12%. Newzoo forecasts mobile +6.8% for the year. The two do not agree. | Partly |
| Spending concentrates in a few franchises | Old games keep earning | GTA is 12.4% of Take-Two's revenue thirteen years after GTA V. 84% of the copies Capcom sold last year were older titles (49.46M of 59.07M). 78% of Take-Two's bookings are spending inside games. | Yes |
| Costs rise for everyone else | Budgets, fees and hardware | Take-Two carries $2.15bn of development cost for unreleased games. Microsoft says memory and storage costs rose more than 2.5 times. US console unit sales in August were the lowest for that month since 2013. | Yes |
| Winners are franchise owners and platforms | Margins show it | Operating margin, last twelve months: Capcom 41.7%, NetEase 35.2%, Tencent 33.3%, Konami 30.8%, Krafton 29.7%. Take-Two -1.4%, Ubisoft and Embracer in loss. | Yes |
| Platforms keep their 30% | The toll is safe | Under pressure. US courts forced Apple to allow outside payment links, and Google is reported to have cut its fee to 20% after settling with Epic. Good for mobile publishers, bad for the two stores. | Weakening |

### 1.3 Validation events that have actually happened

| Date | Event | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 2023 | Microsoft completed its purchase of Activision Blizzard | The largest franchise owners are being taken out of the public market |
| 4 Aug 2026 | Electronic Arts taken private for $55bn by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, Silver Lake and Affinity | A private buyer paid 6.9 times bookings and 21.5 times operating cash flow for a mature franchise owner |
| 2025 to 2026 | Tencent put €1.16bn into Ubisoft's Vantage Studios for about a quarter of it | Even a failing publisher's franchises (Assassin's Creed, Far Cry, Rainbow Six) have buyers |
| 30 Jan 2026 | Google showed its Genie AI world-builder. Unity -24%, Roblox -13%, CD Projekt -8.7%, Take-Two -7.9% in a day | The market fears that making games gets cheap. Nothing has been lost to AI yet. |
| Apr to Sep 2026 | Sony, Microsoft and Nintendo all raised console prices. Switch 2 went from $449.99 to $499.99 on 1 Sep | Hardware gets dearer six years into a cycle, the opposite of every earlier one |
| May 2026 | Nintendo forecast revenue -11.4% and net profit -26.9% for the year to March 2027, citing about ¥100bn of extra cost. Shares fell 8% in a day | The memory shortage reaches profits |
| 25 Jun 2026 | GTA VI priced at $79.99, pre-orders opened | A new price ceiling for big games, set with restraint |
| Jul to Aug 2026 | Roblox guided bookings down 14 to 18% for Q3 and withdrew its full-year forecast after mandatory age checks | A platform's growth can stop for reasons that have nothing to do with games |
| Aug 2026 | Tencent's domestic games revenue +17% in Q2. Capcom reported a ninth straight year of record profit in May | The franchise owners in Asia are doing well |

## 2. Value-chain map

### 2.1 Structure

```
INPUTS AND TOOLS
  Chips and memory        Nvidia, AMD, Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix  (set what consoles and PCs cost)
  Engines                 Unity · Unreal (Epic Games, private)
  Advertising tools       AppLovin · Unity Vector · Google · Meta   (how mobile games find players)

CONTENT: THE FRANCHISE OWNERS
  Western, large budget   Take-Two · [Electronic Arts, private] · [Activision, inside Microsoft]
                          Ubisoft · CD Projekt · Embracer · Paradox
  Japan                   Nintendo · Capcom · Konami · Bandai Namco · Square Enix · Sega Sammy · Koei Tecmo
  China                   Tencent · NetEase · [miHoYo, private] · Century Huatong · 37 Interactive
                          Giant Network · G-bits · Perfect World · XD · Kingsoft
  Korea                   Krafton · Nexon · NCSoft · Netmarble · Pearl Abyss · Shift Up
  Mobile and casual       Zynga (Take-Two) · Playtika · Sea (Garena) · Aristocrat
                          [Scopely, Playrix, Dream Games, Moon Active, Supercell: private or inside Tencent]
  Player-made worlds      Roblox · [Fortnite, inside Epic]

PLATFORMS: THE TOLL COLLECTORS (about 30% of what players spend)
  Console                 Sony (PlayStation) · Nintendo · Microsoft (Xbox)
  PC                      Steam (Valve, private) · Epic Games Store · Tencent WeGame
  Mobile                  Apple App Store · Google Play

HARDWARE AND RETAIL       Logitech · Corsair · Turtle Beach · GameStop
AROUND THE EDGES          Streaming: Twitch (Amazon), YouTube, Huya, Bilibili · Funds: ESPO, HERO
```

### 2.2 Business characteristics by segment

| Segment | How it earns | Operating margin | Structure | Barrier | Cyclical? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Console and PC stores | A cut of every sale, plus subscriptions | High on the store, low or negative on the hardware | Three consoles, one dominant PC store | Installed base, exclusive games | Hardware yes, store less |
| Mobile stores | A cut of every purchase | Very high | Two | Owning the phone | Low, but fee under legal attack |
| Franchise owners, console and PC | Game sales plus spending inside games | 30 to 42% for the best, losses for the rest | A dozen that matter | Franchises and craft | Strongly, around releases |
| Mobile and casual publishers | In-app purchases and ads, after heavy advertising spend | 10 to 25%, falling | Crowded | Weak. Players are rented through ads | Medium |
| Player-made platforms | A cut of spending in worlds built by users | Loss-making on reported profit | Two | Network of creators and players | Medium, plus safety regulation |
| Engines and ad tools | Licences and a share of ad spend | From losses (Unity) to 77% (AppLovin) | Two engines, a few ad networks | Developer habit, data | Follows mobile ad budgets |
| Console hardware | Sold near cost | Thin | Three | Scale | Yes, and now squeezed by memory prices |
| Peripherals and retail | Selling boxes | Low | Fragmented | Little | Yes |

### 2.3 The chokepoints

1. **Franchises that people return to for a decade or more.** There are perhaps twenty in the world. They cannot be built quickly at any price: Take-Two's Zynga purchase ($12.7bn, $5.9bn written off) and Ubisoft's collapse show what happens to those who try to buy or rush them.
2. **The store.** PlayStation had 125 million monthly users in June 2026 and takes its cut of every game, including GTA VI. Sony carries none of the development risk of the games it sells. Steam does the same on PC with a record 42 million simultaneous users, and it is private.
3. **Memory chips.** This is the supply bottleneck of 2026, but the profit from it goes to chip makers. For the games industry it is purely a cost.

The uncomfortable finding: the two best positions in the chain are partly closed to investors. The best store on PC is private, and the largest Western franchise owners after Take-Two have left the market.

## 4. Four-masters analysis of the leaders

### 4.1 Take-Two (TTWO): Information A

Covered in full in the [company report](../Take-Two/Take-Two-research-20261011.md). In short: Rockstar is one of the best assets in entertainment, half the company is a shrinking mobile business bought at too high a price, the shares cost 31 times this year's expected adjusted earnings, and insiders are selling. Base value about $168 against a $213.44 price.

**Rating: ★★★ (watch list). Buy the franchise on a fall, not on the launch.**

### 4.2 Nintendo (7974.T): Information A

- **Business (Duan):** makes its own consoles and the games that sell them. Switch 2 had sold 23.68 million units by 30 June 2026. Operating margin 19.7%.
- **Moat (Buffett):**

| Moat | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Brand / pricing power | ★★★★★ | Mario, Zelda, Pokémon. Games are rarely discounted. |
| Switching costs | ★★★★ | The games exist only on its hardware. |
| Network effects | ★★ | Limited. |
| Scale | ★★★ | Smaller than Sony or Microsoft, enough to run its own platform. |
| Technology / licences | ★★★ | Deliberately modest hardware. |

- **Risk (Munger):** it is the one franchise owner that also carries hardware cost. Management forecasts revenue -11.4% and net profit -26.9% for the year to March 2027, with about ¥100bn of extra cost from memory, tariffs and shipping, and Switch 2 sales of 16.5 million units. If memory stays expensive, the squeeze lasts.
- **Management:** conservative and long-serving, known for cautious forecasts. Cash of ¥1.97tn is 21% of the market value. Grade A.
- **Valuation:** ¥8,146, 44% below its 52-week high. Trailing P/E 19.9, forward P/E 27.9 on the reduced forecast (about 22 once the cash is taken out), EV/EBITDA 13.4, dividend yield 2.0%.

**Rating: ★★★★. The strongest franchises in the industry after a 44% fall, with a fifth of the price in cash. Not cheap on this year's profit, which is the reason it fell.**

### 4.3 NetEase (NTES): Information A

- **Business:** China's second-largest games company, mostly its own franchises (Fantasy Westward Journey, Eggy Party, Identity V, Marvel Rivals). Operating margin 35.2%, return on equity 20.4%.
- **Moat:** franchises that have run for over twenty years in China (★★★★ brand, ★★★ switching costs, ★★★ scale).
- **Risk:** Chinese regulation of games can change without warning. The ADR structure adds legal risk for foreign holders. Results depend on hits abroad.
- **Management:** founder William Ding is the controlling shareholder. Pays a dividend (2.35%). Grade A-/B.
- **Valuation:** $124.24. Trailing P/E 16.5, forward 12.5, EV/EBITDA 8.7, price to free cash flow 10.4. Net cash of about $24bn is 30% of the market value.

**Rating: ★★★★. The cheapest high-margin franchise owner in the scan. The discount is the price of China risk, and that risk is real.**

### 4.4 Tencent (0700.HK): Information A

- **Business:** games were RMB 65.9bn of RMB 204.8bn in Q2 2026 (32%). Domestic games +17%, international flat. Owns Riot (Valorant, League of Legends), controls Supercell, and holds stakes in dozens of studios.
- **Risk:** capital spending rose 190% to RMB 51.8bn in the quarter on AI, and free cash flow was negative RMB 13.8bn. China regulation. Games are a third of the company, so this is not a pure bet.
- **Valuation:** HK$424.8, 36% below its high. Trailing P/E 14.1, forward 12.7.

**Rating: ★★★ (Tier 4). A good way to own a third of a games giant cheaply, if you also want the other two thirds and accept the AI spending.**

### 4.5 Sony (SONY): Information A

- **Business:** PlayStation is its largest division. 95.3 million PS5 consoles, 125 million monthly users, and a cut of every digital sale on the platform. Games profit rose 37% in the April to June quarter, helped by about ¥80bn of tariff refunds.
- **Why it matters for GTA VI:** Sony earns its store fee on every copy sold on PlayStation and on what players spend inside it afterwards, with none of the eight-year development risk. A rough estimate: 35 to 40 million copies, most of them on PlayStation, at $80 with a 30% fee, is several hundred million dollars of high-margin income in a few months. This is reasoning, not a company figure.
- **Risk:** PS5 unit sales fell from 2.5 million to 1.6 million in the latest quarter. The console is late in its life and dearer than at launch. Sony also carries film, music and chip businesses.
- **Valuation:** $24.12, forward P/E 17.7, EV/EBITDA 11.1, price to free cash flow 14.1.

**Rating: ★★★ (Tier 4). The toll collector for GTA VI at about half Take-Two's multiple, diluted by everything else Sony does.**

### 4.6 Capcom (9697.T): Information A

- **Business:** nine straight years of record profit. Sales of about ¥195bn and operating profit of ¥75.3bn in the year to March 2026. Resident Evil Requiem sold about 7 million copies in two months. 84% of copies sold were older games, which cost almost nothing to keep selling.
- **Moat:** franchises (★★★★), its own engine and a disciplined release rhythm (★★★★ scale and craft).
- **Risk:** the price. A weak year for new releases would meet a high multiple.
- **Valuation:** ¥4,393, close to its high. P/E 27.6, forward 27.5, EV/EBITDA 17.3.

**Rating: ★★★ (watch list). The best-run publisher in the scan. Everyone knows it.**

### 4.7 Konami, Krafton, Roblox, CD Projekt: short notes

| Company | Business in one line | Why interesting | Why not | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Konami (Information A) | Football, Metal Gear and card-game franchises with a 30.8% margin and 21% return on equity | Revived old franchises with small budgets; +272% in five years | P/E 27.8 after that run; a third of the business is machines and gyms | ★★★ |
| Krafton (Information B) | PUBG, on PC and mobile | Forward P/E 8.0, EV/EBITDA 4.1, cash equal to 36% of the market value, 29.7% margin | One franchise, about nine years old; -60% in five years; hard to buy from Europe | ★★ |
| Roblox (Information A) | Platform where users build the games | 62% below last year's price; $1.64bn of free cash flow | Bookings falling, guidance withdrawn, $1.0bn net loss, child-safety regulation | ★★ |
| CD Projekt (Information A) | The Witcher and Cyberpunk; 57.9% margin on old games | Two strong franchises, no debt | P/E 45 with the next game targeted for 2028 | ★★ |

### 4.8 Tools: Unity and AppLovin (Information A)

Unity (EV 10 times sales, still loss-making) is recovering because its Vector advertising system is growing 60 to 70% a year. AppLovin (forward P/E 15.4, operating margin 77%) fell 54% in a year after a revenue miss, a short-seller report and a class action. Both are advertising businesses that depend on mobile game budgets. Neither owns a franchise. **Ratings: ★★ each within this theme.** They belong in a report on advertising technology.

### 4.9 The ones to avoid: Ubisoft, Embracer, Playtika (Information A)

All three are cheap on sales and have lost 81 to 92% in five years. Each shows the same thing from a different side: franchises cannot be mass-produced (Ubisoft), cannot be assembled by acquisition (Embracer), and wear out when players are bought through advertising (Playtika). **Rating: ★ each.**

### 4.10 Mainland Chinese companies (Information B)

Giant Network (48.5% operating margin), G-bits (40.2%), Kingnet (34.9%), 37 Interactive (26.2%) and Century Huatong (20.7%) are profitable, and Century Huatong's overseas arm is now the second-largest mobile publisher in the world by in-app revenue. English-language information is thin, the shares are hard to buy from Europe, and this report did not examine them beyond the figures shown. **Not rated.** A short analysis here should not be read as a low opinion.

## 5. Sector-level risk assessment

### 5.1 Systemic risk list

| Risk | Probability | Impact | What to do about it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The chain breaks at "spending per player keeps rising" | Medium | High | Mobile in-app spending is already falling. Prefer companies whose income comes from old franchises, not new players. |
| Memory chips stay expensive and consoles get dearer still | Medium-high | Medium | Micron expects tight supply into 2028 (press reports). Hurts Nintendo and Sony hardware most, publishers less. |
| GTA VI disappoints | Low | High for a few months | It would hit console spending and sentiment across the group. |
| AI makes large games cheap to build | Low for now | Unknown | Franchises and communities are harder to copy than graphics. Engines and outsourcing are most exposed. |
| Regulation: China approvals, loot boxes, child safety | Medium | Medium-high | Already hit Roblox. Keep China exposure to a size you can lose. |
| Store fees fall | Medium | Good for publishers, bad for Apple, Google, and in time Sony and Valve | A reason to own publishers with direct sales to players. |
| Valuation | Mixed | | Japan's quality names and Take-Two are full. China and Korea are cheap. The sector as a whole is not in a bubble. |

### 5.2 Historical analogies

**Hollywood.** Film is the closest older industry: hit-driven, with rising budgets. Over decades the profit went to the owners of libraries and characters (Disney) and to distribution, not to the studios that bet on new films each year. Games are following the same path. Capcom earning 84% of its unit sales from older titles is a library business.

**The industry's own last five years.** This is the more useful evidence, and it is already in the tables above:

| Group | Five-year share price change | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Japan's franchise owners (Konami, Capcom, Bandai Namco, Sega Sammy) | +107% to +272% in yen (about +52% to +173% in euros) | Old franchises, small budgets, steady releases |
| Take-Two, NetEase, Nintendo | +23% to +61% | Good assets, ordinary returns from 2021 prices |
| Growth stories of 2021 (Roblox, Unity, Sea, Krafton) | -32% to -71% | Paying for growth that then slowed |
| Buyers and over-producers (Embracer, Ubisoft, Playtika) | -81% to -92% | Franchises cannot be manufactured |
| HERO fund / S&P 500 | -2% / +78% | Owning the whole sector did not pay |

Most investors in this industry lost money against the market over five years. The winners were not the companies with the biggest launches. They were the ones that spent the least to keep old franchises earning.

### 5.3 Bias check

- **Narrative:** "the biggest launch in entertainment history" is a perfect story. Perfect stories are usually in the price.
- **Anchoring:** many of these shares look cheap against their 2021 peaks. Those peaks were set during lockdowns and are not a reference.
- **Herding:** 28 of 29 analysts rate Take-Two a buy. Almost none write about Krafton or Giant Network in English.
- **This report's own bias:** it rates only what it could read. The Chinese and Korean mid-caps may deserve more than a table row.

> **Munger's question: how does an investor in this industry most often fail?**
> By buying the company with the most exciting game at the moment of greatest excitement. The second most common way is buying a publisher because it is cheap, when it is cheap because it has no franchise.

## 6. Long-run trend (Li Lu)

- **Civilization-level shift or a phase?** Neither. Games became the largest entertainment medium over forty years, and that shift is mostly complete in rich countries. What is left is slower: more spending per player, and new players in India, South-East Asia, Latin America and Africa where incomes are low.
- **Closest historical parallel:** film and television after 1980. A mature medium where technology keeps changing how content is delivered and franchise owners keep most of the value.
- **In 10 to 20 years:** fewer, larger worlds that people stay in for years. Consoles matter less as streaming and phones improve (Take-Two's CEO expects usable low-delay streaming within three years). Store fees end up lower than 30%.
- **Where winner-takes-most is likeliest:** stores on each device, and the top franchise in each genre.
- **What is most likely to be disrupted:** the middle. Mid-size studios, outsourcing, and tools, if AI does to game production what digital cameras did to film crews.

> **Li Lu's question: where does the value sit in 2046?**
> With whoever owns the characters and worlds people care about, and with whoever owns the relationship with the player. Take-Two, Nintendo, Capcom, Tencent and NetEase own the first. Sony, Valve, Apple, Tencent and Nintendo own the second. Everything else in the chain is replaceable.

### Summary table

| Dimension | Conclusion | Confidence |

| Logic chain | Holds, except that mobile spending is flat to falling and store fees are under pressure | Medium-high |
| Best segment (Duan's "right business") | Owners of old franchises with strict budgets | High |

**Verdict: be selective. GTA VI lifts the industry's sales, not its economics. Favour franchise owners with cash (Nintendo, NetEase), use Sony for exposure to the launch itself, and wait for a better price on Take-Two.**

### What the four would say

> **Buffett (simulated):** "In the movie business the popcorn stand makes the money. Here the popcorn stand is the store that takes thirty percent, and the best one on PC is not for sale. So I would look at who owns the characters, and I would want to pay a price that assumes they have an ordinary year."
>
> **Munger (simulated):** "An industry where the index lost money for five years while the product got more popular tells you something. The customers did fine. The shareholders paid for it. Find the three companies that did not need to be heroic to earn forty percent margins and ignore the rest."
>
> **Duan Yongping (simulated):** "Nintendo understands what it is. It makes things people love and it does not chase. NetEase has been doing the right thing in its own market for twenty years. Those are right businesses. Whether the price is right depends on how long you can wait."
>
> **Li Lu (simulated):** "The interesting work here is in Asia, where there are profitable companies at eight to fourteen times earnings that nobody in the West reads about.
