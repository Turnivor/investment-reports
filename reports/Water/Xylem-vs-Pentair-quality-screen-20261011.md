## 1. What this screen does

The screen asks seven yes-or-no questions. Its purpose is narrow: throw out companies that are clearly not first-rate, without throwing out any that are. Passing does not mean "good investment". It means "worth a closer look".

| # | Test | Fail if | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10-year average ROE | below 8% | Does shareholders' money earn more than it costs? |
| 2 | 5-year total free cash flow | negative | Is the profit real cash? |
| 3 | Interest cover (EBIT / interest) | below 2 times | Can it pay its lenders? |
| 4 | Long-run gross margin | below 15% | Is the product differentiated? |
| 5 | Operating cash flow / net income, 5-year average | below 0.7 | Does reported profit turn into cash? |
| 6 | Long-run net margin | below 5% | Is there a cushion when sales fall? |
| 7 | Share count growth over 5 years | above 20%, unless caused by an acquisition | Is management diluting owners? |

Data: SEC XBRL filings for both companies, cross-checked with StockAnalysis. All averages were calculated by script, not by hand.

### Borderline notes

- **Xylem test ① is the one to keep an eye on.** The screen uses a 10-year average, which is still lifted by the pre-Evoqua years. If ROE stays near 8.7%, the 10-year average will drift down toward the 8% line by the early 2030s. The fix is either higher profit on the same equity (margins) or less equity (buybacks, which began in 2026).
- **Xylem test ⑦ is excused, not passed.** The rule forgives dilution caused by acquisitions. Whether the acquisition was worth the dilution is a separate question, addressed in section 4.
- **Pentair's clean pass is backward-looking.** The screen uses data to the end of 2025. It does not see the 2026 pool-channel correction, the guidance cut or the new debt. None of those would change the result, but they are the reason the share price halved.

**The single most telling line is free cash flow per share.** Xylem's total free cash flow rose 42% in five years, but there are 35% more shares, so each share earns only 5% more cash than in 2020. Pentair's rose 49% per share over the same period. Xylem grew the company. Pentair grew the owner's share of it.

The counterpoint: Pentair's 2025 figures were inflated by distributors buying ahead. Its 2026 free cash flow per share will be lower. Xylem's 2026 figures are still rising.

## 5. Conclusion

- **On the quality screen, Pentair is the better business by the numbers.** Higher returns on capital, higher margins, no dilution, better growth in cash per share.
- **On stability and trust, Xylem is ahead.** Its customers are utilities, not homeowners. Its problems in 2026 are slow growth and an expensive habit of acquisitions, not a broken forecast.
- **On price, Pentair is clearly cheaper** and offers more return per dollar in every scenario tested, with a similar percentage downside.
- **The two shares are down for different reasons.** Xylem's fall is mostly the market no longer paying 25 times earnings for 2% growth. Pentair's fall is an earnings shock and a loss of confidence in management.

**In one line: Xylem is the steadier business at a fair price. Pentair is the more profitable business at a low price, with a management team that has to win back trust. Neither is a clear bargain today, and neither looks like a company to give up on.**

## 6. Limits of this screen

Passing the screen does not make a company a good investment. The screen does not look at the price, the durability of the business model, the quality of management or the direction of competition. Those are covered in the two research reports. Specific limits here:

- Pentair's figures before 2019 are distorted by the nVent spin-off.
- Xylem's adjusted earnings changed definition in 2023, so GAAP figures were used for every test.
- Post-deal leverage and interest cover for both companies are estimates, because neither acquisition is yet in a published balance sheet.
- Star ratings in section 4.5 are judgments, not measurements.
