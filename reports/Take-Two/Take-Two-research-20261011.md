## 2. The business: Duan Yongping's "right business"

**One sentence:** Take-Two owns a handful of entertainment franchises (Grand Theft Auto, Red Dead Redemption, NBA 2K, Borderlands, and Zynga's mobile puzzle games) and earns more from what players spend inside the games than from selling the games.

### 2.1 Where the money comes from (fiscal 2026)

| By platform (net bookings) | $m | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile | 3,300.9 | 49% |
| Console | 2,687.0 | 40% |
| PC and other | 733.1 | 11% |
| **Total** | **6,721.0** | |

| By label (management's February 2026 estimate for the year; not reported afterwards) | Share | Main products |
|---|---|---|
| Zynga | about 46% | Toon Blast, Match Factory!, Empires & Puzzles, Color Block Jam, Words With Friends |
| 2K | about 38% | NBA 2K (2K26 sold over 12 million copies), Borderlands 4, WWE 2K, Civilization |
| Rockstar Games | about 16% | GTA V and GTA Online, Red Dead Redemption 2 |

Other facts from the annual report:

- **Recurrent consumer spending** (virtual currency, add-on content, in-game purchases, subscriptions) was **78% of the total, about $5.2bn**. Full-game sales are the small part.
- **Grand Theft Auto was 12.4% of revenue** in fiscal 2026, thirteen years after GTA V came out. The five largest franchises were 54.3%.
- 97% of revenue is digital. 59% comes from the United States.
- The five largest customers (Apple, Sony, Google and Microsoft each above 10%) carried 80.6% of revenue. They are the shops, and they keep roughly 30% of what players pay.
- 12,909 employees, 9,998 of them in development studios.

**What fiscal 2027 guidance says about GTA VI.** Management expects the label mix to move to Rockstar 37%, Zynga 34%, 2K 29%. Rockstar's share of an $8.1bn midpoint is about $3.0bn, against about $1.08bn last year. The increase of roughly $1.9bn, divided by the $50 to $55 that a publisher keeps from an $80 digital sale (an estimate; the platforms take about 30%), is **35 to 38 million copies** between 19 November and 31 March. That is in line with what analysts read into the guidance (JPMorgan: mid-30 millions) and below most of their own forecasts (Piper Sandler 35 million, Morgan Stanley 40 million, Bank of America 45 million in the first year, Morningstar 60 to 70 million). For comparison, GTA V has sold over 230 million copies in thirteen years.

Two things stand out. First, **$2,149.7m of development spending on unreleased games sits on the balance sheet** ($11.50 a share), most of it GTA VI by any reasonable reading. It becomes a cost when the game sells. Second, the more GTA VI earns, the more Rockstar's staff are paid through internal royalties. Shareholders do not keep all of a hit.

### 2.3 Is it a good business?

**What is good about it**

- **One of the best assets in entertainment.** GTA V kept selling for thirteen years, and Grand Theft Auto still brings in about an eighth of the company's revenue. Very few products of any kind do that.
- **Spending inside games repeats.** 78% of bookings come from players who are already there.
- **NBA 2K is a dependable annual product** with growing engagement (daily players up 15%, in-game spending up 7% in the latest quarter).
- **Little physical capital.** Capital spending is 2 to 3% of revenue.
- **The price of GTA VI was set with restraint**: $79.99 when some analysts had argued for $100. That protects volume and goodwill.

**What is not so good**

- **The gap between hits is thirteen years.** Rockstar releases one game roughly every five to eight years. Between them, results depend on the online mode and on the other two labels.
- **Half the business is mobile, and it is shrinking.** Mobile bookings fell 7% in the latest quarter and are guided down for the year. Zynga cost $12.7bn and $5.9bn of it has been written off.
- **Little of the revenue reaches shareholders as cash.** In its largest year ever, Take-Two guides to "over $1bn" of operating cash flow on about $8.1bn of bookings (about 12%). Electronic Arts produced $2.55bn on $8.03bn (32%) in its last year as a public company.
- **Shareholders have been diluted, not paid.** No dividend, no buyback since fiscal 2022, 62% more shares.
- **The shops take a third and can change the terms.** Console prices are also rising: Sony and Microsoft raised them again in 2026, and US console unit sales in August were the lowest for that month since 2013 (Circana, via press reports).

> **Duan Yongping's question: what is good about this business, in one sentence?**
> It owns a franchise that people will pay for again thirteen years after they first bought it. The addition a careful owner has to make: one franchise carries the value, the other half of the company was bought at too high a price, and so far the people who work there have been paid better by the success than the people who own it.

## 3. Moat: Buffett's "economic moat"

| Moat type | Test | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand / pricing power | Can it raise prices without losing volume? | GTA is the strongest brand in games: over 465 million copies sold in the series. GTA VI is priced at $79.99, above the $69.99 standard, and pre-orders were described by the CEO as "unprecedented and astonishing". NBA 2K has pricing power inside the game but pays a licence. Zynga has none. | ★★★★★ Rockstar · ★★★ 2K · ★★ Zynga |
| Switching costs | How costly is it to leave? | Years of progress, characters and purchases in GTA Online and NBA 2K. Low in mobile puzzle games. | ★★★ |
| Network effects | Does it get better with more users? | Online worlds are better when friends are there. Real, but weaker than in a social network. | ★★★ |
| Scale | Does size lower cost or raise the bar? | Few companies can fund a game for eight years with $2bn or more of capitalised cost. That is a barrier, and also a concentration of risk. | ★★★★ |
| Technology / licences | Is it protected? | Rockstar's own engine and craft are hard to copy. The NBA licence must be renewed and gets more expensive. | ★★★ |

**Trend:** at Rockstar the moat is as wide as it has ever been, and GTA VI should widen it. At 2K it is stable. At Zynga it is narrow and getting narrower as competitors outspend it on advertising, which management itself said on the last call.

> **Buffett's question: will the moat still be there in ten years, and what could destroy it?**
> Grand Theft Auto will almost certainly still matter in 2036. What could damage it is a launch that damages trust (the way Cyberpunk 2077 did for CD Projekt), a loss of the people who make it, or tools that let many more studios build large open worlds cheaply. None of these looks likely today. The moat is not the problem with this investment. The price is.

### 4.2 Historical analogies

1. **Take-Two after GTA V (2013).** The business did what a hit-driven business does: revenue +94%, then -54%. The shares did well anyway ($18.17 at the end of September 2013, $23.07 a year later, $28.73 a year after that). The reason matters. In October 2013 the whole company was valued at about **$1.76bn, or 0.75 times that year's revenue**. Today it is valued at $39.9bn, **4.9 times** this year's guided bookings. In 2013 the market paid almost nothing for what came after the launch. Today it pays for a great deal of it.
2. **Take-Two after Red Dead Redemption 2 (October 2018).** The game was a large success. The shares went from $137.99 at the end of September 2018 to $87.26 at the end of February 2019, down 37%, in a weak market and amid worry about competition from Fortnite. A hit does not protect a full price.
3. **CD Projekt and Cyberpunk 2077 (December 2020).** The share price peaked before release and fell by more than two thirds after a launch with serious technical problems (general knowledge, not re-verified for this report). Rockstar's record on quality is much better, which is why this path is rated low.

### 4.3 Bias check

- **Narrative:** "biggest launch ever" is true and already known to every buyer and seller.
- **Anchoring:** the 52-week high of $265.94 and the average target of $286 are not values. The target is roughly 28 times one peak year's adjusted earnings.
- **Herding:** 28 buys, no holds. When everyone who can be convinced already is, good news moves the price less than bad news.
- **What the bears say:** there is one sell rating. Morgan Stanley's own downside scenario is $170. The cautious argument is the one in this report: fiscal 2028 is priced as a new normal, when it may be a peak.
- **What the bulls say:** guidance is conservative (it was about 11% below analyst estimates when given), the installed base of consoles is over 130 million, the online mode and a later PC version extend the cycle for years, and the forward multiple of about 25 times is the lowest in a year (12-month average: about 44 times).

> **Munger's question: where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would smart people not buy?**
> Smart people would not buy because they are being asked to pay about 24 times the earnings of what may be the best year of the decade, after stock pay, while the chief executive sells. The likeliest way this caution is wrong: the new online world earns two or three times what GTA Online earns, and fiscal 2028 turns out to be a floor. That is possible. It is not what the company's history shows, and the price leaves no payment for the risk.

### 5.2 Alignment

- **The top two executives are not employees.** Zelnick and president Karl Slatoff are provided by ZMC Advisors under a contract that runs to 31 March 2029. ZMC is paid a $3.3m annual fee, a bonus of up to $13.2m, and share awards. In fiscal 2026 the cost was **$79.5m** ($16.6m in cash, $62.9m in shares).
- **How the bonus is set.** It depends only on an adjusted EBITDA figure that leaves out share pay, bonuses and acquisition charges. The fiscal 2026 target was $919.5m, the result was $1,401.6m, and the maximum was paid. The share awards depend 75% on share price performance against the NASDAQ-100 and 25% on recurrent consumer spending.
- **Ownership is small.** All directors and officers together hold 1.12% of the company, and most of that is unvested units held through ZMC. Zelnick's family trust held 152,314 shares after his August sales, worth about $32.5m.
- **Insiders are selling.** On 10 August 2026, three trading days after the Q1 results, Zelnick's trust sold 30,000 shares at $250.49 to $255.05 and a charitable trust sold 10,000 more (Form 4; no trading-plan box ticked). Aggregator sites count 57 insider sales and no purchases in six months, including about 339,000 shares by Zelnick and about 249,000 by Slatoff. Many follow the yearly vesting of awards on 1 June, which makes some selling routine. The same sites show sales by Zelnick in early March 2026 too. No insider bought at any point in the period.
- **A large outside holder.** Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund owns 6.10% (11.4 million shares). The same fund led the $55bn purchase of Electronic Arts that closed on 4 August 2026.

> **Duan Yongping's question: if the CEO retired, would the company stay competitive?**
> Yes, because the value sits in Rockstar and 2K, not in the head office. The harder question is whether the head office spends the GTA VI money well. The record is one very good decision (patience with Rockstar), one expensive one (Zynga), and a habit of paying in shares. An owner should watch what is done with the cash in 2027 more closely than the launch itself.

## 6. Industry and long-run trend: Li Lu's "civilization" lens

- **Is this a civilization-level shift?** No. Games are a large, mature form of entertainment. Newzoo puts the 2026 market at $213.9bn, up 6.1%: mobile $121.1bn, console $46.9bn, PC $45.9bn. The number of players grows about 3 to 4% a year. Growth now comes from spending per player.
- **GTA VI matters to the whole industry.** Newzoo says console revenue would fall in 2026 without it.
- **The value chain squeezes the middle.** Platform owners take about 30%. Hardware is getting dearer because memory chips are short. Development budgets have risen faster than prices. The companies that earn well are the ones with a few franchises people return to for years. Take-Two has one of the best of those and a mobile arm that does not qualify.
- **Consolidation sets a price.** Microsoft bought Activision, and Electronic Arts was taken private at $55bn: 6.9 times bookings and 21.5 times operating cash flow. Take-Two at $40.6bn is 5.0 times this year's bookings but about 40 times this year's guided operating cash flow. On sales it looks cheaper than EA. On cash it looks twice as expensive.
- **Customer and supplier concentration:** four platform companies carry 80% of revenue.

More in the companion [industry report](../industry/VideoGames-industry-20261011.md).

> **Li Lu's question: looking back from 2046, is this the Standard Oil of its era or a 3Com?**
> Neither. It is closer to a film studio that owns the most valuable series ever made and releases an instalment once a decade. Such a company can be worth a lot. It is rarely worth the multiple of a business whose earnings arrive every year.

## 7. Valuation and margin of safety

### 7.1 What the market is paying today

**History (StockAnalysis, fiscal year-ends):** forward P/E was 24.5 (March 2022), 26.0 (2023), 46.7 (2024), 31.9 (2025), 32.0 (March 2026) and is 26.0 now. Those multiples were paid on earnings before the launch. A multiple paid on launch-year earnings should be lower, because the best year is no longer ahead.

**Peers (StockAnalysis, 9 Oct 2026), forward P/E:** Krafton 8.0 · NetEase 12.5 · Tencent 12.7 · Sony 17.7 · Konami 23.5 · **Take-Two 26.0** · Capcom 27.5 · Nintendo 27.9 · CD Projekt 42.8. Operating margins over the last twelve months: Capcom 41.7%, NetEase 35.2%, Tencent 33.3%, Konami 30.8%, Krafton 29.7%, Nintendo 19.7%, Sony 13.7%, Take-Two -1.4% (GAAP).

### 7.2 Reverse check: what does $213.44 assume?

For a buyer to earn 10% a year for three years with no dividend, the share must be at **$284** in October 2029.

- At 22 times earnings, that needs adjusted EPS of **$12.91** in fiscal 2030, 25% above the consensus for the launch-cycle year of fiscal 2028.
- At 26 times, it needs **$10.93**, a little above fiscal 2028.

So the price assumes one of two things: profits keep growing more than 10% a year after the best launch in the company's history, or investors still pay a pre-launch multiple once the launch is behind them.

### 7.3 Three scenarios over three years (`financial_rigor.py three-scenario`)

Starting point: consensus adjusted EPS of $6.87 for fiscal 2027. End point: fiscal 2030 (April 2029 to March 2030), which is what the market will be looking at in October 2029.

| Scenario | EPS growth a year | Fiscal 2030 EPS | P/E | Price in Oct 2029 | Change vs $213.44 | Value today (discounted at 10%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull: record launch, online mode earns well above GTA Online, PC version follows | 25% | $13.42 | 26x | $348.9 | +63.4% | **$262.1** |
| Base: strong launch, earnings hold near the fiscal 2028 level as online spending replaces game sales | 14% | $10.18 | 22x | $223.9 | +4.9% | **$168.2** |
| Bear: good launch, then a slump as after GTA V; online mode slow, mobile shrinking | 0% | $6.87 | 16x | $109.9 | -48.5% | **$82.6** |

Why these multiples: 22 times adjusted earnings is about 25 times earnings after share pay. It is a full price for a company after its launch, used here as the middle case. (For comparison, the buyers of Electronic Arts paid 21.5 times its operating cash flow.) The bull case keeps today's multiple. The bear case uses the multiple of an ordinary publisher between hits.

**The base case discounted to today is about $168, 21% below the current price. The bull case is 23% above it and the bear case 61% below.** A 25% margin of safety on the base case is about **$126**.

### 7.4 Ten-year discounted view (`terminal_value.py`)

Exit multiple from the perpetual-growth formula only: exit P/E = (1 - g / ROIC) / (r - g). No peer multiples.

**The three inputs and why**

| Input | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| r, cost of capital | **10%** (USD) | Framework default for dollar cash flows (range 9 to 11.5%). |
| ROIC on new capital | **15%** | A judgment between two extremes. Games built in-house earn very high returns (StockAnalysis shows ROIC of 42.6% in fiscal 2022, before Zynga). Acquisitions have earned little: $12.7bn paid for Zynga, $5.9bn written off. |
| g, growth after 2036 | **3%** base (2% bear, 4% bull) | Long-run nominal US growth. Capped at 4% for USD. |

**Audit result:** all three hard checks passed (currency match, r minus g of at least 5 points in every case, discrete risks kept out of the discount rate). Denominator width: 8, 7 and 6 points.

Starting profit: fiscal 2028 consensus adjusted net income of about $1,955m ($10.29 × 190M shares) less about $271m of share pay after tax, giving **$1,684m**. Market cap $39,909m.

| Case | Profit path to fiscal 2037 | Fiscal 2037 profit | Exit P/E | Cash returned a year | 10-year return a year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | Falls below the fiscal 2028 level | $1,300m | 10.8x | 1% | **-8.9%** |
| Base | +4% a year from fiscal 2028 | $2,400m | 11.4x | 2% | **-1.7%** |
| Bull | +8% a year from fiscal 2028 | $3,370m | 12.2x | 2.5% | **+2.8%** |

**Sensitivity to r (base case):** at r = 8% the exit P/E is 16.0x and the return is +1.6% a year (bull +5.6%, with g held at 3% to keep the denominator at 5 points). At r = 12% the exit P/E is 8.9x and the return is -4.1%. The conclusion does not change sign anywhere in the range: the base case stays far below a 10% hurdle at every rate tested.

**What it would take.** For 10% a year with 2% returned in cash, the company must be worth about $86bn in 2036. At the formula's 11.4 times that is $7.6bn of profit. Even at 22 times it is $3.9bn, against $1.7bn expected in fiscal 2028.

This test is strict on purpose. It refuses to assume that the market will still pay a premium multiple in ten years. Under that rule the shares do not pay for themselves at today's price.

### 7.5 Limits of this valuation

- **Not modelled:** generative AI lowering the cost of making large games, regulation of in-game purchases or platform fees, and the loss of key Rockstar staff. All three are flagged by the audit tool as outside the model.
- A delay or weak reception, a slow online mode and expensive consoles are handled as scenarios (the bear case), not through the discount rate.
- Adjusted EPS is the analyst consensus. Take-Two does not publish it. The deduction for share pay ($330m a year, 18% tax) is this report's estimate.
- Cash flow will look better than earnings in fiscal 2028 and 2029, because about $2.1bn of past development spending will be charged against profit without any cash leaving. That is a real plus for the balance sheet and is not captured in an earnings multiple.
- Fiscal 2030 is three and a half years away in a hit-driven business. The range between the bear and bull cases ($83 to $262) is the honest measure of how little is known.
- The bull case may be too low if the online world becomes a platform in its own right. The bear case may be too mild if the launch itself goes wrong.

> **Buffett's question: if r is wrong by two points, does the conclusion flip?**
> No. At 8% the base case earns 1.6% a year, at 12% it loses 4.1%. The conclusion would flip only by assuming that buyers in 2036 pay well over 20 times earnings. That is an assumption about other people's future optimism, not about the business.

> **Duan Yongping's question: if the market closed for five years, would you hold at this price?**
> For the franchise, gladly. At this price, no. With the market closed there is nothing to live on: no dividend, no buyback, and a cash yield of about 1%. The whole return would have to come from someone paying more in 2031.

### In your money: what this means in lei

- **Price:** one share $213.44 ≈ €190.26 ≈ 1,016 lei; the base value of $168 ≈ €150 ≈ 800 lei; the margin-of-safety level of $126 ≈ €112 ≈ 600 lei. National Bank of Romania reference rates of 9 Oct 2026: 1 EUR = 5.3414 lei, 1 USD = 4.7612 lei.
- **Return:** the base case is -1.7% a year over ten years in dollars, in a range of -8.9% to +2.8%. A return in lei is that figure plus or minus the change in the lei price of the dollar. Between 26 Feb and 9 Oct 2026 the leu lost 10.2% against the dollar and 4.8% against the euro (BNR reference rates), which shows how much the exchange rate alone can move a lei result.
- **Inflation:** Romanian inflation was 6.2% a year in August 2026 (INS). The National Bank of Romania forecasts 6.1% for end-2026 and 3.4% for end-2027. None of the three ten-year cases beats either figure. Over three years, only the bull case (+63% in dollars) clearly does.
